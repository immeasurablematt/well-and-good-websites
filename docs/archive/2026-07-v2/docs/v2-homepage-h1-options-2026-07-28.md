# Homepage H1 Options for Well & Good Growth

**Date:** 2026-07-28  
**Brand lock:** Always **Well & Good Growth**  
**Job of the H1:** Name the buyer’s problem or outcome fast. Not explain the whole offer.  
**Primary CTA under it:** Get a free website prototype

---

## Current v1 baseline

**H1:** Your next customer is Googling you, and calling someone else.

**Why it works**
- Clear pain in one breath
- No jargon
- Works above any single trade
- Makes the cost of inaction obvious

**Why it can be beaten**
- Slightly long
- “Googling” is a little dated when ChatGPT/Claude also matter, but still plain
- Names the problem, not the turnaround

**Verdict:** Strong baseline. Only replace it if the alternative is clearly sharper.

---

## Recommended winner

### Option A (recommended)

**H1:** Your next customer found someone else first.

**Subhead:** Well & Good Growth builds owned websites for local businesses, then grows the searches that turn into real enquiries, appointments, and sales.

**Why this is better than v1**
- Same idea, fewer words
- Harder landing
- Still brand-level, not mechanic-only
- Leaves room for Google, maps, and answer engines in the subhead

**CTA pair**
- Primary: Get my free website prototype
- Secondary: See website plans

---

## Strong contenders

### Option B

**H1:** Get found. Get chosen. Get the call.

**Subhead:** Owned websites and local growth for businesses that already earn trust, but lose customers to whoever shows up first online.

**Why it works**
- Punchy, rhythmic, easy to scan
- Maps cleanly to website then growth
- Close to older umbrella language without saying “operate better”

**Risk**
- A little more slogan-like than concrete

### Option C

**H1:** If they can’t understand you online, they call the next business.

**Subhead:** Well & Good Growth turns your services, reviews, and next step into a clear website, then grows the local searches that fill the calendar.

**Why it works**
- Very plain
- Points at clarity and conversion, not just “being online”
- Good fit for trades and professional services

**Risk**
- Longer than A

### Option D

**H1:** Strong reviews. Weak website. Lost work.

**Subhead:** Well & Good Growth builds the owned site that makes your reputation usable when a customer is ready to choose.

**Why it works**
- Extremely scannable
- Matches the real pipeline condition: highly reviewed, weak owned web presence
- Good for demo-first selling

**Risk**
- Staccato style may feel abrupt on a premium page

### Option E (keep v1, lightly tightened)

**H1:** Your next customer is searching, and calling someone else.

**Subhead:** Well & Good Growth builds the website that earns the call, then grows the local searches behind it.

**Why it works**
- Keeps almost all of the original line
- “Searching” ages better than “Googling”

---

## Do not use

| Line | Why not |
|---|---|
| Get found first. Earn the call. Book more repair work. | Too segment-specific for the brand homepage |
| Websites, SEO, and AI operations for growing businesses | Mechanism soup, weak desire |
| The digital partner local businesses trust | Generic agency fog |
| Affordable custom websites in 24 hours | Offer detail, not brand H1; can live in subhead/proof |

---

## Pairing rules

Whatever H1 wins:

1. Brand in logo/nav is always **Well & Good Growth**
2. Subhead states Websites first, Growth second
3. Prototype remains the primary CTA
4. Segment links come later on the page, not in the H1
5. No em dash characters in page copy
6. No ranking, citation, or revenue guarantees in the hero

---

## My pick

**Use Option A.**

**H1:** Your next customer found someone else first.

**Subhead:** Well & Good Growth builds owned websites for local businesses, then grows the searches that turn into real enquiries, appointments, and sales.

**Primary CTA:** Get my free website prototype  
**Secondary CTA:** See website plans

If you want maximum continuity with the live site, use **Option E** instead.
