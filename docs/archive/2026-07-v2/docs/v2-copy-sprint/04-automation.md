### A separate service line by design

Automation has a different buyer, discovery process, pricing model, and proof base than Websites and Growth. It begins by mapping one process, the tools already involved, the failure cases, and the point where a human needs to approve the work.

The capability: remove one repetitive workflow while keeping human approval at risk points.  
The benefit: time back, fewer dropped balls, and a process that does not depend on memory.

### What can be a fit

- Lead intake, sorting, and routing
- Research and recurring reporting
- Inbox and CRM preparation
- Structured document generation
- Meeting follow-up and action capture
- Knowledge organization and retrieval
- Monitoring and recurring internal processes

Some steps need simple rules or integrations. Variable inputs may benefit from AI within defined limits. The system should match the work, not force a trendy tool into it.

### Human approval stays where it matters

Judgment, money, permissions, customer communication, and irreversible actions deserve a person in the loop. The goal is to prepare and route routine work, then pause for approval wherever risk is real.

### Start with one workflow

A useful first conversation identifies the work that happens often enough to matter, takes real time, follows a recognizable pattern, or creates avoidable mistakes. If the process is rare or judgment-heavy from start to finish, automation may not be the right answer. That should be clear before a system is proposed.

### Proof, labelled correctly
