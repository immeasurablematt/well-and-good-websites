### When one page is the right call

A focused page fits when the business has one primary offer, one location, and one action that matters most: calling, booking, requesting, ordering, or visiting.

It can carry:

- The offer, and who it is for
- Services or package detail
- Reviews, photos, and business proof, placed next to the action rather than filed on a separate page
- Hours, location, and contact details
- The questions customers ask before they act
- A call, booking, request, or contact path

### When you need more room

Choose a multi-page site when different services need their own explanation, when several locations matter, when the business needs deeper proof, or when different customer groups need different routes through.

The goal is never to add pages. It is to give the customer enough to choose you, using the fewest pages that do the job.

### It can still grow

A focused page is a real foundation, not a placeholder. If you later need service pages, local search content, or conversion work, growth builds around what is already there.

No page count guarantees a ranking. A one-page site is not inherently better or worse for search than a ten-page one. What matters is whether the page carries clear services, real business details, structured data, and useful local signals.
