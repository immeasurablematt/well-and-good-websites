# Two paths. Pick the one that matches the problem.

Websites and growth follow one path. Automation follows another. The point is to start where it would make the biggest difference, not to sell you all three.

**CTA:** Tell me what is not working

### The website path

**1. Send two things**
Your business name and a current Google, social, or website link. I build a private prototype from what is already public, including your own photos rather than stock images of somebody else's shop.

**2. Look at it, then decide**
Review the direction before any money changes hands. If it is right, we confirm the services, proof, photos, policies, and the action you want customers to take.

**3. Launch**
The site goes live with your services, reputation, location, and next step clear on a phone.

**4. Add growth if it earns its place**
Once the foundation is live, growth expands into the local searches and services worth pursuing. It is a separate decision, not a condition of buying a website.

[See Websites and published pricing](`/services/websites/`)
[See Growth](`/services/growth/`)

### The automation path

Automation starts with one repetitive workflow. The first conversation maps the current process, the tools and people involved, the permissions, the ways it goes wrong, and every point where a person needs to approve what happens next. Only then does a system get scoped.

Depending on what has to be monitored, it may be a fixed project or an ongoing system. Its price has nothing to do with website packages.

[See Automation](`/services/automation/`)

### What is free, and what is not

The website prototype is free, with no obligation and no call required to get it.

The first automation call is free. If the process turns out to need deeper investigation before anything can be honestly scoped, that discovery becomes its own scoped paid piece of work, agreed in advance. You will not find that out after the fact.

### What determines the cost

Website prices are published in full. Growth depends on your services, your geography, how competitive the searches are, and how much gets published. Automation depends on the workflow steps, the systems, the permissions, the failure risk, and how much oversight it needs.

[See published website pricing](`/services/websites/`)

### Questions worth answering

**Can I just buy a website?**
Yes. A one-time build is a complete purchase. Growth gets added only if it serves an actual business goal.

**What if automation is not a fit?**
If the work is rare, or it needs judgment at every step, automating it is not worth your money. You should hear that on the first call rather than after a scoping fee.

**What if I already have a website?**
Send the link. The prototype shows what a rebuild would look like, which is a more useful thing to argue about than an audit.
