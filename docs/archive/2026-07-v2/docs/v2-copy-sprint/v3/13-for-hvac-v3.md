### What the site has to do in that moment

Three things, immediately and on a phone:

- **Confirm you do this work.** Repair, maintenance, installation, replacement. The visitor needs to see their problem named.
- **Confirm you cover them.** Your service area, in the words people use for where they live.
- **Make the next move obvious.** One clear way to reach you, visible without scrolling.

Everything else on the page is supporting evidence: real photos of your trucks and your people rather than stock images, reviews placed next to the contact path instead of filed on their own page, and the practical details a homeowner wants before picking up the phone.

Get those right and more of the right local customers choose you instead of the company whose site was simply easier to understand.

### Built from your business, not a template

I will not publish a service, a claim, a licence, a financing statement, or an availability promise that you have not confirmed. That sounds obvious. It is also the opposite of how most templated contractor sites get built, which is why so many of them describe a company that does not quite exist.

The structure gets shaped by your actual service mix, coverage area, scheduling process, and policies. Where those are still open questions, we settle them before anything goes live rather than publishing a maybe.

### After the foundation is live

Growth builds around the services and local searches worth pursuing: service page work, Google Business Profile improvements, conversion improvements, analytics, and call or request tracking that shows which pages are producing customers.

How much it moves depends on your market, your site, your service mix, and the work that gets done. Search positions, citations, appointments, sales, revenue, and timing are not guaranteed by anyone honest.
