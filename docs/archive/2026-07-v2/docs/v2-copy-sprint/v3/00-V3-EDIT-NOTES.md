# V3 Copy Edit: Diagnosis and Decisions

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Scope rewritten:** homepage, websites, growth, automation, auto-repair, web-design-niagara, affordable-website-design
**Scope noted only:** how-it-works, work, about, contact, one-page, hvac, thank-you
**Delivered as:** `*-v3.md` alongside the originals. Nothing was overwritten.

The v2 pack passed its own verification gate cleanly and the constraint discipline in it is real. What follows is not a compliance problem. It is a selling problem.

## Three decisions to approve before implementation

**1. First person.** The v3 pages say "I build" where v2 said "Well & Good Growth builds." The brand string still appears exactly as **Well & Good Growth** in every title, meta description, and at least once in body copy on every page, so the locked naming rule holds. But a practice whose central claim is "one person accountable for the work" cannot write about that person in the third person without undercutting the claim on contact. This is the single largest change in the pass and it is fully reversible.

**2. The locked outcome phrase is now used once per page, not repeatedly.** "More qualified enquiries, appointments, and sales" is a definition from the brief. In v2 it appeared on six pages, twice on some. Repeated that often it stops reading as a promise and starts reading as a template. It is retained where it does work: the Growth hero, and the Growth section of the auto-repair page.

**3. Growth pricing is now addressed on the Growth page.** v2 left growth cost entirely unmentioned in the reader-facing copy on a site whose main differentiator is published pricing. The v3 section does not invent a number. It says what the price depends on and that it is quoted after the first conversation, which is honest and stops the silence reading as concealment.

## What was wrong, in priority order

**The strongest offer in the business was buried and written like a disclosure.**
"Send your business name and I will build you a working website, free, before you pay anything" inverts how every web agency sells. On the v2 homepage it sat in section seven, below proof and pricing. On six pages it was described in near-identical process language: "a private website prototype from your public services, reviews, business details, and publicly available photos where available." That sentence hedges twice and dramatizes nothing. In v3 the offer moves to section two on the homepage and second on Websites, and it is written as a trade: you send an email, you get a website to look at, you owe nothing.

**Thin proof was disclaimed instead of used.**
The proof discipline on this site is unusually honest and it should stay exactly as strict. But v2 spent more words on what the proof was not than on what it was. One live client, two concepts, and prior consultancy is a short list. It is also the precise reason the free prototype is the right lead: with no case studies to lean on, the answer is to do the work first and let the prospect look at it. v2 never made that connection. v3 closes the homepage proof section with it.

**Internal strategy vocabulary was printed on the page.**
"The capability: turn more local searches into qualified enquiries, appointments, and sales. The benefit: a steadier flow of appropriate new business." That is a matrix row, not a sentence. It appeared on Websites, Growth, Automation, HVAC, and three times on auto-repair. No customer has ever wondered what the capability is. The content survives in v3; the labels are gone.

**Hedging was applied to deliverables, not just to outcomes.**
"Tracking foundations as appropriate." "Photos where available." "Chosen to fit the offer." "Google Business Profile connection and local-search structure support the site" followed by "where useful." The no-guarantees rule requires hedging *results*: rankings, citations, appointments, revenue, timing. It does not require hedging what is in the box. When a deliverable is hedged, the reader assumes they might not get it. v3 states deliverables flatly and keeps every outcome qualified.

**The two intent landings did not earn their own existence.**
`/web-design-niagara/` used the word Niagara six times and made no local argument, so it read as the Websites page with a place name dropped in. It now leads on the only genuinely local thing available: a named person in Welland who answers the phone. `/affordable-website-design/` repeated that prices are published instead of answering the three fears behind a price search. It now runs on those three questions, all of which the pack already had answers for.

**The best insight on the site was in paragraph three of section two.**
The auto-repair page's point about phone price-shopping and why a diagnosis cannot be quoted blind is the most trade-specific, most useful, most differentiated argument anywhere in the pack. It is now the section directly under the hero.

**One continuity break.**
The term that automation discovery may become scoped paid work appeared only on `/engagements/`. A visitor converting from `/services/automation/` met it for the first time after enquiring. It is now stated on the Automation page next to the CTA it affects.

## Notes on the seven pages not rewritten

**`/contact/` has the most valuable unfixed problem in the pack.** The offer everywhere is "send two things." The form asks for seven, including "What should customers do on your site, or what workflow do you want to improve?" and "Anything else we should know?" That is a promise made on every page and broken at the point of conversion. Recommendation: make name, business name, contact, and link required, and everything else optional or on a second step after submission. This is likely worth more than any wording change in this document.

**`/about/`** has the same third-person problem as the homepage, and it hurts most here, because the page's entire argument is personal accountability. "More than a decade" of search and content experience is a real credential currently sitting inside a disclaimer block; it can lead the section and still be labelled accurately.

**`/work/`** is conceptually the strongest page in the pack and needs only the same closing move as the homepage: end by pointing at the prototype, since honest thin proof is an argument *for* looking at the work rather than an apology for not having more. Separately, Evelyn's Sandwich Factory is a food concept, and food is out of scope per the brief. It is labelled correctly and is not a vertical claim, but it spends a prospect's attention on a market that has been de-prioritized, and it appears on the homepage in front of auto-repair and HVAC visitors. Consider replacing it with a second service-business concept.

**`/engagements/`** is sound and is the longest overage against the word targets. The free-call and paid-discovery terms must stay consistent with the Automation v3 wording. The CTA "Find my starting point" names no action; "Tell me what is not working" or a straight route to Contact would convert better.

**`/one-page-websites/`** is fine as written. Low stakes, correctly positioned as a package.

**`/for/hvac/`** is honestly hedged, and the notes correctly say not to publish before validation. One publication-blocking detail: "Possible paths to confirm during discovery" is a planning artifact addressed to the reader. Whatever survives validation should be stated as a service list, and anything unvalidated should be cut rather than published as a maybe.

**`/thank-you/`** is fine and is the one page with an unused opportunity: someone who has just converted is the best audience on the site for a second, low-cost ask, such as sending photos or naming the three jobs they want more of. Adding one would speed up every prototype.

## Constraint check on the v3 files

- Homepage H1 matches the locked string exactly, character for character
- Zero em dashes, zero en dashes, zero exclamation points across all seven files
- Brand string appears only as **Well & Good Growth**, no variants
- Every ChatGPT mention is paired with Claude
- All pricing tokens preserved: $297, $497, $997, $1,497, $39, $99 + $497, $399 + $997, $899 + $1,497, $990, $3,990, $8,990, 12%, $250, $300
- No prohibited audience-size wording
- Every instance of the word "guarantee" is a negation
- Proof labels intact: live website client, concept, prior consultancy, internal demonstration
- No invented client, testimonial, result, statistic, or timeline

## Still needs a human

- Confirm the yearly plan figures ($990, $3,990, $8,990) against the current `site-v2/services/websites/` page before publishing. These carried through from v2 and were not in the original brief.
- Decide on first person before implementation, since it affects every page including the eight not rewritten here.

## One lesson worth keeping

Hedge outcomes, state deliverables. The no-guarantees rule is about results you cannot control: rankings, citations, appointments, revenue, dates. It says nothing about what is in the box. When "as appropriate" and "where available" creep from the outcome sentences into the deliverable sentences, the copy stops sounding careful and starts sounding evasive, and the reader quietly concludes they might not get the thing.

# Addendum: pack complete (2026-07-30)

All 14 route pages now have a v3. The seven that previously had notes only have been rewritten to those notes, plus findings from a practitioner-source research pass on what converts on local service websites.

## What changed in the final seven

**`/contact/`** - The form went from seven fields to four plus a dropdown. This was the highest-value unfixed problem in the pack: every page promises "send two things" and the conversion point demanded seven. The open-ended questions are now one optional box, and the useful detail they collected is better gathered in the reply than demanded up front. An "After you send" section was added.

**`/about/`** - First person throughout, which matters most here because the page's whole argument is personal accountability. The decade of Jetta Grove experience now opens its section with the accuracy label immediately after, instead of being buried inside a disclaimer block. "Founder-led" is gone as a phrase; the page describes the operating model instead of naming it.

**`/work/`** - A CTA now sits directly under the live client rather than only at the bottom, since proof placed next to an action outperforms proof filed on its own. A new closing section turns the short evidence list into the argument for the free prototype instead of leaving it as an unaddressed weakness. Evelyn's Sandwich Factory is retained here because this page is a complete inventory, but it sits behind JK Motors and should not be promoted to the homepage; food remains out of scope as a vertical.

**`/engagements/`** - CTA changed from "Find my starting point", which named no action, to "Tell me what is not working". The free-versus-paid automation terms are now their own labelled section, worded identically to `04-automation-v3.md` so the two cannot drift.

**`/one-page-websites/`** - Light pass. Reframed so a focused build reads as a fit decision rather than a budget compromise, which protects the price. Added the point that proof belongs beside the action.

**`/for/hvac/`** - The v2 page published a section headed "Possible paths to confirm during discovery" followed by a list of maybe-services, which is a planning artifact pointed at the customer. All of it is cut. The page now names only the four service categories safely true of HVAC as a category and converts the uncertainty into a promise: nothing gets published until the company confirms it. Still not voice-of-customer research, and the validation list before launch is unchanged and still binding.

**`/thank-you/`** - Two deliberate changes. The v2 note said "No response timeline is promised", which applied the no-guarantees rule to the wrong kind of claim: when you reply to an email is entirely within your control, and it is the one question every person who just submitted a form is asking. `[VERIFY]` marks the commitment. Second, the page now makes an optional second ask for photos, because someone who has just converted is the most willing they will ever be and real business photos directly improve the prototype that closes the sale.

## Research corroboration

A practitioner-source pass produced three findings that fed these rewrites. All come from agencies selling home-services web design, so their self-published statistics are unverified and the two sources disagree on benchmarks. The qualitative guidance is convention worth following, not evidence.

- Published pricing is supported: "Even rough ranges or clear explanations reduce anxiety. Silence creates suspicion." This also confirms that Growth's unpriced silence in v2 was the real liability on a site whose pitch is no quote theatre.
- The ten-field contact form is treated as dead across both sources, and a stated response expectation is treated as baseline.
- Stock photography is treated as actively credibility-destroying, and real jobsite and team photos as the differentiator. The prototype offer uses the prospect's own photos, which was a selling point sitting unused inside a process description.

## Open items before implementation

1. **First person** still needs a yes or no. It now runs through all 14 pages and reversing it is a whole-pack change.
2. **`[VERIFY]` on the thank-you page.** Put in the response time you will genuinely hit every time. If that is two business days, say two.
3. **Yearly plan figures** ($990, $3,990, $8,990) still need checking against the live `site-v2/services/websites/` page.
4. **HVAC validation** is unchanged and still blocks that page's publication.
5. **Style sheet.** Cross-page drift exists and is documented but not yet resolved: mixed straight and curly typography in three v2 files, `local-search` versus `local search` inconsistent within single files, four variants of one-page, and no documented rule for when Websites / Growth / Automation are proper nouns. Worth settling before the HTML build so the rules hold in implementation.
