## Decision

Reposition the existing business under a broader umbrella rather than launching a separate venture. And, new in v2: decouple the repositioning from the renaming.

- **Reposition now:** message, offer architecture, site architecture, schema.
- **Rename later, only if evidence demands it:** legal name, wordmark, domain migration.

**Working umbrella name:** Well and Good Growth Systems (working direction only, pending Phase 0 availability checks)
**Interim display brand:** Well and Good, with the descriptor "Websites, search visibility, and AI-powered operations"
**Domain:** stay on wellandgoodwebsites.ca (see Phase 4)

This gives the business room to sell:

1. Websites as an accessible entry offer
2. SEO and AEO as recurring growth services
3. Agentic operations automation as the highest-value offer
4. Catholic Web Stewardship as a specialized vertical (parked; see Open Decisions)

A separate automation company would split attention, proof, domain authority, and lead generation before there is evidence that buyers need separate branding. The automation practice can be spun out later if it develops a distinct enterprise audience.

### Naming notes (new in v2)

- The live site, CLAUDE.md, and all existing copy use "Well and Good" (no ampersand). Pick one form and use it everywhere: legal name, copy, schema, wordmark. Recommendation: "and" in copy and legal, ampersand at most as a wordmark styling decision.
- "Growth Systems" is the weakest part of the working name: it is the kind of vague label this repo's own copy guidance warns against, and a Welland trades owner does not search for "growth systems." It is acceptable as a working umbrella because the descriptor line carries the real meaning. Validate it against real buyers before committing legally. Candidate alternatives to check at the same time: Well and Good Growth, Well and Good Digital.
- Dropping "Websites" from the *displayed* brand (Well and Good + descriptor) removes the anchor immediately, with no legal or domain cost. That buys time to let the rename decision be made on evidence instead of urgency.

## Why the Current Site Is Limiting the Business

The problem is larger than a few displayed prices. The site currently positions Well and Good as the inexpensive website option:

- The hero sells "80% less than agencies charge" (`index.html:204`) and "live in 24 hours" (`index.html:210`).
- The agency price comparison table anchors the whole company at "From $99/month" (`index.html:341-358`).
- The main offer ladder tops out at $899 per month (`index.html:398-455`).
- The contact form forces prospects into those plans (`index.html:504-511`).
- Structured data tells search engines and AI agents the company's range is $99 to $1,497 (`index.html:51`), with per-plan `Offer` descriptions repeating every dollar figure (`index.html:67-125`).
- Site-wide meta, Open Graph, and Twitter descriptions all lead with "from $99/month" (`index.html:7-18`).
- The sitemap contains only four website-oriented pages. There is no SEO, AEO, automation, or operations page.

A qualified automation buyer could reasonably conclude that the company builds cheap local websites, not that it can redesign a revenue or operations workflow.

### What the current site does well (do not lose this)

The site converts on a proven, low-friction entry funnel: free website preview, 24-hour build, published prices, and honest proof (Frank Baggetta as a live client, Jetta Grove as track record, concept builds labeled as concept builds). The repositioning must not zero out this funnel before the new one exists and is measured. Several Phase 1 items below are scoped specifically to protect it.

## Two-Track Offer and Pricing Architecture

This section replaces v1's "remove every public dollar figure."

There is a real tension the v1 draft skipped past. The competitor research proved that published pricing is the one differentiator no local competitor matched, a conversion advantage, and an SEO asset for price-intent queries ("website design toronto prices", "local seo pricing niagara"). But company-level price anchors cap the perceived ceiling for automation buyers. Both are true. The resolution is scoping, not scrubbing:

### Track 1: Entry offers keep published prices, on their own pages only

Website builds and website-plus-growth plans keep published pricing, but only on the pages that sell them: `/affordable-website-design/`, `/one-page-websites/`, `/web-design-niagara/`, and the future `/services/websites/`. `Offer` schema lives on those pages, scoped to those specific services.

Why this is safe for positioning: a machine-readable price attached to a page describing a specific entry product teaches agents "this company offers starter websites from $497," not "this company costs $99 per month." It is the sitewide `priceRange` and homepage anchoring that flatten the company to its cheapest product.

Why it is necessary: quote-gating the exact audience that converts on transparency hands the wedge back to competitors, and forces the owner to hand-quote every small website lead. Track 1 stays productized and self-serve.

### Track 2: The umbrella and high-value offers carry no dollar figures

Homepage, `Organization` schema, site-wide meta and Open Graph, and the SEO, AEO, and automation service pages publish no prices. Remove `priceRange` entirely; do not replace it with a higher range. Search, AEO, and operations engagements are scoped work.

Do not leave the old prices in a hidden `pricing.md`, `pricing.txt`, structured-data block, or other machine-readable file at the company level. That would keep teaching agents that the company is a $99-per-month provider.

### The How Engagements Work page

Replace company-level dollar figures with a transparent **How Engagements Work** page covering:

- **Website projects.** Clearly scoped builds for organizations that need a stronger foundation. Lightweight projects remain available when that is genuinely all the client needs (link to Track 1 pages and their published prices).
- **Search growth.** Ongoing SEO, local visibility, content, and AEO programs based on market competition, geographic scope, and publishing requirements.
- **Operations systems.** Discovery, implementation, and ongoing improvement for workflows where automation can recover time, reduce mistakes, or increase capacity.

Pricing-related questions to answer without publishing company-level anchors:

- What determines the cost?
- Do you offer project and retainer work?
- Can you start with one workflow?
- Are affordable websites still available? (Yes, link to them.)
- What is included in discovery?
- When does automation produce a worthwhile return?

This stays clear and extractable for AI systems while preserving the ability to scope a lightweight website, a search engagement, or a larger automation system appropriately.

## AEO Strategy for the Repositioned Site

The site should demonstrate the AEO work the company sells.

### Authoritative service pages

Each service page opens with a concise, self-contained definition, then provides examples, limitations, implementation details, and evidence. Priority questions:

- What is AEO?
- How is AEO different from SEO?
- What is agentic operations automation?
- Which business processes can AI agents automate?
- How much human oversight should an AI workflow have?

### Topic clusters (publish by intent, at a sustainable cadence)

Target cadence: two articles per month, highest buyer intent first. Do not open the Insights hub with stubs.

**AEO and AI visibility:** AEO vs SEO · How businesses appear in ChatGPT and Claude answers · Local SEO for AI search · Schema for AI visibility · How to measure AI citations · What `llms.txt` does and does not do · AI visibility audit methodology

**Agentic operations:** AI automation vs traditional automation · Processes that should not be automated · Human approval in agentic workflows · Automating lead intake · Automating meeting follow-up · Connecting AI agents to existing software · Security and account-access practices · Calculating automation ROI

**Websites and conversion:** When a one-page site is enough · Website redesign vs targeted improvements · SEO migration requirements · Local service website architecture · Conversion paths for service businesses

### Trust signals

Every insight article includes: author (Matthew Baggetta), relevant experience, published and updated dates, sources for external claims, original screenshots or workflow diagrams, clearly explained methodology, and links to related services and case studies.

Proof constraint (from CLAUDE.md, non-negotiable): the only real proof assets today are Frank Baggetta and the Jetta Grove track record. Never fabricate results, testimonials, or client outcomes. Leave placeholders rather than invent.

Schema: updated `Organization`, `Service`, `Article`, `FAQPage`, and `BreadcrumbList` schema, with `Offer` data scoped per the Two-Track rules. The current company-level `Offer` and `priceRange` data is removed as part of the Phase 1 scrub.

## Implementation Sequence

### Phase 0: Baseline and validation (new; do this first, it is a few hours)

Success: baseline saved; name cleared or replaced.

### Phase 1: Remove company-level price anchors

Scope: homepage, `Organization` schema, site-wide meta and Open Graph, navigation, and thank-you messaging. Not the Track 1 acquisition pages.

- Remove every dollar figure from the homepage.
- Remove company-level `Offer`, `price`, `priceSpecification`, and `priceRange` schema.
- Replace "Plans" navigation with "Services."
- Remove "80% less than agencies charge."
- Remove the agency price comparison table.
- Replace the plan selector in the contact form with a needs selector: Website · SEO and search visibility · AEO and AI visibility · Operations automation · Not sure yet.
- Homepage CTA becomes the strategy call; the free website preview CTA stays on the Track 1 pages.
- Update titles, meta descriptions, Open Graph copy, Twitter copy, CTAs, FAQs, and thank-you messaging so no company-level pricing or narrow positioning remains.

Success: no dollar figures at the company level; Track 1 page lead volume holds within roughly 20% of the Phase 0 baseline over the following four weeks. If entry leads crater, the rollback is a git revert, and the two-track scoping gets revisited before proceeding.

### Phase 2: Establish the umbrella positioning

- Keep the existing visual identity initially; change the displayed wordmark to "Well and Good" plus the descriptor.
- Write the new homepage around the three capabilities.
- Create the four service pages and the How Engagements Work page.
- Update About and Contact.
- Create `.agents/product-marketing.md` as the canonical positioning context, and write it **before** the new page copy so every future copy task (and the copywriting/CRO skills that read that file) works from one source of truth.

Success: a first-time visitor reading only the homepage can name the three capabilities and does not conclude "cheap website company."

### Phase 3: Build proof for the high-value offer (moved up; starts in week 1, in parallel)

v1 sequenced proof at Phase 4, after the homepage already led with automation. That launches the flagship offer proofless. Instead:

- Fastest honest proof: document the business's **own** agentic systems as case studies (the AI-assisted build-and-publish pipeline, research and reporting workflows, AI-visibility monitoring), labeled as own-business systems exactly the way concept builds are labeled on the current site. This is real, demonstrable, and available immediately.
- Then produce one or two client automations. Candidates: lead intake to qualification and follow-up · meeting transcript to summary, tasks, and CRM updates · recurring research and reporting · inbox or document processing.
- Each case study shows: the original manual process, the system architecture, where human approval remains, tools and integrations used, time or error reduction, and the business result.
- Until this proof exists, the automation page describes the offer and the own-business demonstrations. No claims the proof cannot support, nothing invented.

Success: two published automation write-ups with real numbers, at least one from a paying client within two quarters.

### Phase 4: Preserve search equity (no domain migration now)

Do not discard `wellandgoodwebsites.ca`, and, stronger than v1: do not schedule a migration at all yet. The site's rankings are young and the display-brand change already removes the anchor. A domain migration is high risk for near-zero current benefit.

- Keep the existing domain active and canonical.
- Keep `/affordable-website-design/`, `/one-page-websites/`, and `/web-design-niagara/` as focused acquisition pages, reframed under the larger company instead of deleted.
- Domain migration becomes an optional, evidence-gated final step, only after a legal rename: one-to-one 301 redirects, updated canonicals, schema, sitemap, Search Console, profiles, and citations.

Success: acquisition-page rankings and impressions hold through the repositioning.

### Phase 5: Publish the authority layer

- Add `/llms.txt` with accurate company and service context.
- Expand the XML sitemap.
- Implement `Service`, `Organization`, `Article`, `FAQPage`, and `BreadcrumbList` schema per the Two-Track scoping.
- Add author identity and updated dates.
- Publish direct-answer FAQs.
- Build the AEO and operations topic clusters at the two-per-month cadence.
- Check target queries monthly across Google, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude.
- Record citation, mention, and recommendation rates separately.

## Risks and Mitigations (new in v2)

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Entry lead flow drops after the homepage de-anchoring | Track 1 pages keep prices and the preview CTA; compare weekly against the Phase 0 baseline; rollback is a git revert |
| Flagship ops offer launches without proof | Phase 3 runs in parallel from week 1; ops copy stays within what own-business demos support |
| Rename is dead on arrival (name, domain, or trademark conflict) | Phase 0 checks happen before anything depends on the name |
| One-person capacity cannot ship twenty pages | MVP architecture; hubs deferred; two articles per month cap |
| Quote-gating burns owner hours on small website leads | Track 1 stays productized and self-serve with published prices |
| Two audiences (trades owner vs ops buyer) muddle the homepage | Hero leads with the shared outcome; the needs selector and service pages split intents within one scroll |

## What Not to Do

- Do not launch a second full company and maintain two sites now.
- Do not make "AI" the business name. Tools and terminology will change.
- Do not lead with cheap websites, even though they remain available.
- Do not call automation a vague "AI transformation" service.
- Do not bury websites entirely. They are a useful entry product and visible proof of execution.
- Do not remove published pricing from the entry-offer pages. It is the one differentiator no local competitor matched, and the top of the funnel.
- Do not migrate domains before a legal rename exists and redirects, canonicals, and the new information architecture are ready. Default is no migration.
- Do not retain old company-level machine-readable pricing after removing visible pricing.
- Do not ship the new architecture as thin stub pages. Launch a page when it can be the best available answer to its question.
- Do not produce separate thin content "for AI." Write useful content for people and organize it clearly enough for search and answer engines to extract.
