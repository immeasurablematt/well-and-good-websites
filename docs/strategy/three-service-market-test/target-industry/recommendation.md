# Target industry recommendation

Prepared September 26, 2026 by Claude desktop at Matthew's request ("start from first principles to find me the right target"). Status: recommendation for review. No one has been contacted and the site is unchanged.

Inputs: `criteria.md` (what must be true, and the knock-out screen of about 40 industries), `research/` (three parallel research reports covering 8 shortlisted industries in Canada and the US, each with a 10-site website sample), and `../data/keyword-sample-verticals.csv` (customer and owner search data, US$0.18).

## Answer

**Target independent, owner-run wedding and event venues in Canada and the US:** barns, estates, farms, halls, and winery or inn event spaces.

**Runner-up: small manufacturers and machine or fabrication shops (about 5 to 100 staff).** Choose it instead only if technical capability content for engineers does not feel like the B2B work you want to leave behind.

## Why venues, from first principles

1. **One customer pays for the website.** The average US wedding venue fee was US$12,900 for 2025 weddings (The Knot, 10,474 couples). Even a rural raw-space venue in the sample starts at US$4,800. One extra booking covers a US$3,000 to 5,000 site.
2. **They already spend money on marketing that they do not own.** Venue marketing vendors report directory spend of US$500 to 1,200 a month in competitive metros, often on 12-month contracts (low-quality sources, but consistent). A site that converts those leads, and brings some directly, is an easy argument next to that spend.
3. **The gap is visible from outside, and it is specific.** None of the 10 sampled venue homepages showed a starting price, and only 2 showed capacity. Outreach can open with "couples can't tell what you cost or how many guests fit," which is checkable in two minutes.
4. **It rewards what you are good at.** The purchase is considered and comparison-heavy: 90% of wedding planning happens online, 82% of couples book the venue first, 36% used AI in planning (nearly double the year before), and 52% said responsiveness built trust. Clear copy, pricing clarity, search and AI visibility, and fast enquiry follow-up are exactly the skills you want to sell.
5. **Owners can say yes, and there are enough of them.** About 72% of roughly 35,800 US wedding venues are single-owner (directory scrape, low quality); a venue owners' Facebook group claims 7,300+ members. Canadian counts are unknown, so the US carries the volume.
6. **The other two services follow naturally.** Enquiry intake and tour-booking follow-up (automation), then local and AI search (growth marketing). All three service lines stay intact.
7. **Your existing work is the closest proof you have for any industry.** Frank's site already has an event enquiry form asking date, venue, and guest count, and serves weddings and corporate events. Same buyer, same form logic. It is unpaid family work, so present it as a portfolio piece, not as a paying client.

## Why not the others

- **Design-build contractors:** best economics (US median renovation US$20,000), but 9 of 10 sampled renovators already had strong sites, and specialist agencies (US$12,000 to 24,000) plus local agencies crowd it.
- **Event performers:** best proof match, but most are solo operators averaging about US$28,000 in annual receipts, and marketplace listings cost US$129 to 479 a year. They cannot comfortably spend US$3,000 to 5,000. Treat Frank's site as a bridge into venues instead.
- **Wineries:** the Niagara story is real, and the WineDirect Classic shutdown on December 31, 2026 forces rebuilds, but alcohol-marketing rules, commerce-platform integrations, established wine agencies, and a shrinking industry make it a poor first niche. Niagara winery event spaces can still be included in the venue list.
- **Accounting:** easy to reach and high client value, but 57% of clients come by referral, template vendors sell good-enough sites for US$85 to 170 a month, and tax season blocks selling for four months.
- **Auto repair and detailing:** vertical platforms (auto repair) or thin budgets and cheap templates (detailing).

## Runner-up detail: small manufacturers

- About 24,000 US and 4,300 Canadian establishments with 5 to 99 staff across machine shops, structural metals, and plastics alone (Census CBP 2023; StatCan Business Counts 2025).
- 3 of 10 sampled shops had no website; most others were functional but vague ("custom machining"), with no drawing upload on the quote form.
- Specialists split into cheap templates (US$749 to 2,499) and big programs (US$50,000+ a year); the strategy-plus-copy middle looks thin.
- Weaknesses: small advertising budgets (about CAD 6,500 a year for a typical small Canadian machine shop, ISED 2024), relationship-driven buying, and no transferable proof yet.

## Risks with venues, and what would change the answer

| Risk | What would show it | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Owners are content with directory leads and see a site as secondary | Replies say "our leads come from The Knot" | Reframe the offer around converting directory leads, not replacing them |
| Venues already on Showit or Squarespace with good photos (3 of 10 sampled were strong) | Many prospects fail the visible-gap check | Filter the list to venues missing price or capacity before contacting |
| Weak photography | Prospects cannot supply usable images | Qualify on existing photographer galleries before quoting |
| Seasonality | Owners are busiest through fall and do not reply | Build the list now; owners buy in their quieter winter season |
| Wedding demand flat, some new venues fail | Prospects are struggling financially | Prefer venues with several years of reviews |
| Samples were small and biased toward marketing-active businesses | Real prospects look different | The first 40-venue list is itself the better sample |

If 40 well-targeted venue contacts produce fewer than 3 replies after one message revision, move to the runner-up.

## First test (about 3 selling hours a week, one project at a time)

1. **List 40 owner-run venues:** about 20 in the US (pick one or two states), 10 in Ontario outside Niagara, and 10 in Niagara, including winery event spaces where you have warm contacts. Keep the list private (for example `~/Hermes/Workbench/market-test/`).
2. **Check each in about 15 minutes:** starting price shown, capacity by space, what the enquiry form asks, mobile problems, and whether ChatGPT or Claude names them for "wedding venues near [town]".
3. **Send one short personal note per venue** with two or three specific findings and an offer of a free homepage preview. Do at most three previews. Canadian emails need CASL consent (a conspicuously published address with no "no solicitation" notice, and a message relevant to their business, with records kept); US emails follow CAN-SPAM (accurate sender, physical address, opt-out). Nothing is sent without your go-ahead.
4. **Offer:** a venue website rebuild at about US$3,500 or CAD 4,500, 50% deposit. Scope: pricing guide, spaces and capacity, packages, FAQ (catering, rain plan, lodging), galleries, an enquiry form that captures date, guest count, and budget, tour booking link, Google Business Profile, and AI-search foundations. Later add-ons: fast-reply enquiry follow-up (automation), and search work (growth marketing).
5. **Track per venue:** contacted, replied, preview made, proposal, paid, hours spent, country, warm or cold, reason for any no.
6. **Decide after the 40 contacts:**
   - 1 or more paid builds: keep venues.
   - 5 or more replies but no sale: change one thing (price, scope, or the opener) and contact 20 more.
   - Fewer than 3 replies: rewrite the opener once and test 20 more, then try the venue owners' community or directories as a channel before switching to manufacturers.

The site stays as it is during this test. If two or more venues pay, that is the evidence for a later, separately approved change to the site's positioning.
