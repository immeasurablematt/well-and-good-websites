# Cross-line challenge: three-service market study

Prepared 2026-09-25 by the independent challenger. Inputs: research-websites.md, research-growth.md, research-automation.md, the two keyword CSVs, plan.md. Extra web research: four read-only lookups (listed in section 6), no metered APIs, no contact. Labels: [A] assumption, [NE] no evidence found, "arith" for arithmetic on sourced inputs.

## 0. Headline

The draft's primary is not really "growth marketing." It is a new offer (B2B strategic content and fractional retainer) that does not appear on the public site, sold through a network whose size is unknown, to a buyer segment whose funding base is shrinking in count, priced with benchmarks from a different job (fractional marketing leadership). The public growth packages (local SEO, social, GBP) have the weakest evidence of anything studied. The draft borrows the line's name to carry a different offer.

That does not make the B2B direction wrong. It makes it a hypothesis that should be gated by one cheap measurement (network depth and reply rate) before it is called the sales priority.

## 1. Steelman: websites as primary

Evidence the draft under-credited:

1. **Only line with a completed client under this brand.** Frank Baggetta: search clicks 25 to 51 per 28 days, client-reported 3 enquiries in month one. Growth's proof is prior consultancy and employment work; automation's is one internal example. The plan (Phase 1) says not to borrow proof across lines. Under that rule, websites is the only line with in-brand client proof.
2. **The whole storefront is already built for it.** Site, public prices, free preview, local identity. The plan freezes all of these during the test. Any other primary has to sell around a site that points somewhere else.
3. **Price headroom locally, not a price war.** Every Niagara comparator with published prices sits above WGG's setup fees: Niagara Web Design CAD 1,750 + CAD 50/mo; Vision and Cool Koala "from CAD 2,500", typical CAD 3,500 to 8,000 (research-websites s3). WGG can raise one-time build prices in a private offer and still be at or below market.
4. **Professional builds on DIY platforms are growing, not dying.** Wix Partners revenue +17% in Q2 2026 vs +15% total; USD 469M (2023) to 750M (2025) (research-websites s2e). AI builders may shift work to professionals using them rather than removing it (Circle designers using AI report 21% higher revenue).
5. **The channel evidence is as strong as growth's.** Admin Bar 2026 (n=622): word of mouth is the primary lead source; agencies that cultivate it are 53.8% consistently profitable vs 33.1% passive. This is the same kind of generic "referrals dominate" evidence the draft uses for fractional work (Fractional Jobs 94%).
6. **Recurring component exists.** Launch/Grow are setup plus monthly, and Admin Bar ties 25%+ recurring revenue to far lower odds of being unprofitable (under 10% vs 58.3%). The website line is not only one-time fees.
7. **Bounded delivery and fastest proof loop.** A website finishes; a content retainer's outcomes take months (Google: "four months to a year" for SEO, research-growth s6).

Where the steelman is weak: Niagara web-design search is about 340/mo across three terms with 5+ local competitors; the average Circle designer grosses about USD 50,000/yr; grant-driven demand (CDAP, Digital Main Street) is closed; 55% of no-presence firms say digital is not relevant (CFIB). The ceiling looks low unless prices rise.

## 2. Steelman: automation as primary

1. **It is the only line with growing transaction evidence.** Upwork Q2 2026: total GSV down 4%, AI-related GSV up 22%, AI Strategy and Consulting GSV up more than 50% (SEC 8-K, research-automation s2). Websites and growth show flat or shrinking low-end transactions (Fiverr buyers down, Yelp flat, Thryv marketing services down 62%).
2. **Fastest-growing buyer intent in official data.** StatCan Q3 2026: 25.2% of employer businesses plan to use AI in the next 12 months, up from 14.5% a year earlier and 10.6% two years earlier. No comparable official growth signal exists for buying websites or marketing.
3. **Largest ticket per engagement among local-accessible offers.** Canadian single-workflow builds CAD 5,000 to 25,000; audits CAD 1,500 to 2,500; retainers CAD 500 to 10,000+/mo (Flowgrammer, Clarity, ChatGPT.ca). A CAD 7,500 build is two to three local websites.
4. **The draft used the worst denominator to dismiss it.** "10.7% of AI-using 1 to 4 staff firms" is the micro band. The same StatCan table gives 30.2% for AI-using 100+ firms, and CFIB shows 60%+ GenAI use and 62% AI investment at 20 to 49 staff. The draft itself says to test automation with 10 to 99 staff firms, then cites the 1 to 4 figure against it.
5. **Asymmetric evidentiary burden.** Automation was penalized for having a purchase-rate statistic (StatCan measured it). Growth has none: "No primary source found that measures the share of Canadian or US SMBs that pay an outside provider for SEO, social or content" (research-growth s2a). Absence of evidence was treated as neutral for growth and presence of low evidence as negative for automation.
6. **"No proof" applies to growth too.** The B2B proof is prior consultancy work, and some of it is third-party estimates (io.net "est. organic visits 5,097 to 27,022"). An automation demo built on a prospect's own workflow is arguably as persuasive.
7. **Same network, same buyers.** The founder's B2B contacts (10 to 99 staff, SaaS stacks) are the segment with the highest AI adoption (information sector 39.7% in US BTOS). If the network is the channel, automation can ride it as easily as content.
8. **Maintenance drag is not unique.** A monthly content retainer is also a recurring obligation; social scope creep is flagged as high (research-growth s6).

Where the steelman is weak: local search volume for automation is effectively zero; national Canadian terms are small (ai consultant 1,000/mo, and "ai consulting" is the same grouped bucket, not additive); OECD shows more SMEs cutting contractors (14%) than adding (6.3%); no measured ROI; two priced competitors already market to Niagara at CAD 1,000 to 2,500 entry points; the Upwork growth is global and includes development work.

## 3. Attack on the B2B-network primary

**3.1 Identity and sunk-cost bias.** The recommendation is, in substance, a return to the prior consultancy (Jetta Grove track record, 10+ years B2B tech). The study does not know why the founder moved to a local website business. If the reasons were demand drying up, burnout, or wanting local, owner-facing work, those reasons still apply and the recommendation fails on fit, not on market data. This is the single most important unasked question. The researcher who likes the evidence most is also the one whose evidence matches the founder's past, which is exactly the pattern a challenger should distrust.

**3.2 Funding environment (tested, section 6).** The buyer the draft names is "early-stage VC-backed" (36% of fractional hiring, Fractional Jobs). In 2026 that pool is shrinking in count while dollars concentrate:
- Crunchbase (2026-07-02): over 70% of Q2 2026 global startup capital went to AI companies; 16 companies took 53% of the quarter; OpenAI and Anthropic took 43% of H1 funding. Crunchbase also reports seed and angel dollars down 27% year over year and deal counts at a multiyear low (search summary of the Q2 report).
- Canada: CVCA Q1 2026 had 104 deals, the lowest quarterly count since 2017 (BetaKit, 2026-05-13). CVCA Q2 2026 page summary: seed CAD 285M across 82 deals, down 31% in dollars year over year; H1 deal count down 8.8% (274 to 250). The page's own figures do not fully reconcile, so treat as directional.
- Read-across [A]: fewer funded non-AI startups means fewer "funding round" triggers, the main trigger listed for fractional leadership (research-growth s1). AI startups are funded, but a solo marketer with no AI-company proof is not their obvious hire. io.net is AI infrastructure, which partly offsets this.

**3.3 Does the fractional evidence generalize?**
- **Wrong job priced.** USD 209/hr is the average for fractional marketing roles, which skew to leadership. The report gives no split between content execution and CMO-level work. The draft sells "strategic content and positioning" then a "content/fractional retainer." Content execution is the most AI-commoditized part of marketing: Verblio USD 0.06 to 0.16/word; Demirci et al. show 21% fewer writing job posts after ChatGPT. Pricing a content retainer from a leadership benchmark is the most favorable assumption in the draft.
- **Wrong population.** FRAK 2024 is all functions (finance, ops, etc.), US, n=250, and 29.5% of respondents were under USD 5,000/mo. Fractional Jobs is US-only, a platform community, and surveys working fractionals (survivorship: people who failed to land clients are less likely to be in the sample or describe themselves as fractional).
- **Supply is enormous.** Fractional Jobs: about 150,000 US fractionals; platform has 44,433 member profiles against 1,447 postings from Jan 2024 to May 2026. Arith: about 31 member profiles per posting on that platform. This is a crude, platform-internal ratio, not a market clearing rate, but it contradicts reading "149% YoY hiring growth" as easy demand.
- **"94% won clients through referrals" is not a conversion rate.** It says referrals are how fractionals get work, not that a given network produces work.

**3.4 Network exhaustion.** Network size, seniority, recency, and warmth are all unknown. Network channels typically produce a first wave then decay [A]. The draft has no estimate of how many contacts hold budget authority today. Ten-plus years of startup work also means many contacts' companies have since been acquired, shut, or moved on [A].

**3.5 Conflict with the local brand.** A B2B software buyer who checks wellandgoodgrowth.ca sees local-business packages at CAD 199 to 1,299/mo. Two risks [A, magnitude NE]: (a) credibility mismatch ("websites for local businesses") and (b) price anchoring, since a USD 5,000+/mo proposal from a firm advertising CAD 699/mo bundles invites the question of what the difference buys. Counterpoint [A]: fractional buyers hire a person via LinkedIn and referrals, and may never look at the site. The plan freezes the site, so this confound is guaranteed and must be recorded per prospect, not assumed away.

**3.6 Concentration risk.** At USD 5,000 to 8,000/mo, one to three clients could carry most revenue. Startup clients end engagements when funding runs out or a full-time hire lands (most engagements 6 to 12 months). Losing one client could remove a large share of revenue at once; losing one of fifteen Launch clients would not. The draft counts the upside of large retainers without pricing this.

**3.7 Territory claim.** "Starting in the GTA, inside the stated service territory" is technically true, but the evidence for the segment is US-weighted (Fractional Jobs US-only, FRAK US, Contra US startups). The GTA framing keeps the recommendation compatible with the brand without the evidence being GTA evidence.

**3.8 Counterevidence the draft applied to local SMB but not to B2B.** Ahrefs: about 58% lower CTR for top pages when AI Overviews appear on informational queries (research-growth s6). B2B content marketing lives on informational queries, so it is hit harder than "service + city" local search, where Whitespark found AI Overviews on only 15% of simple local-intent queries. The draft used growth's counterevidence to escape local SMB, then did not apply it to B2B content.

## 4. Local vs beyond local

**Against expanding beyond local:**
- No non-local client proof in any line except the Toronto website client (who is inside the GTA territory anyway).
- US search volume (7.5 to 11x Canada) is irrelevant if the channel is network, not search. The draft uses network as the channel but the US size argument quietly relies on search volume.
- US markets have far larger supplier bases (150,000 US fractionals; many US productized web and automation shops).
- Tax and admin: zero-rating of exported services is plausible but service-specific conditions were not verified (research-websites s8).

**Against staying local:**
- Local inbound is tiny across all three lines: web design about 340/mo; SEO about 100/mo; automation about zero (keyword CSV).
- Local SMB budgets are flat to contracting in the best public data (Yelp paying locations down 1%, "challenging environment"; Yext smaller customers contracting).
- Local price floor collapse in marketing (CheckSite CAD 30 to 55/mo; Localo USD 39 to 49).
- Solo web designer average gross about USD 50,000/yr (Circle, self-report).

**What neither side has:** any measure of whether local identity raises close rates (NE in all three files). Both "local advantage" and "local is a liability for B2B" are unmeasured.

| Issue | Where | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Retainer vs one-time fee | Draft compares fractional USD 5,000 to 8,000/mo against website CAD 2,500 to 4,000 one-time | Compare on cash collected minus direct costs per total founder hour over the same window, with churn applied. A website plus care plan or Launch monthly is also recurring |
| Currency mixing | USD 209/hr vs CAD 100/hr Grow benchmark | Arith: USD 209 is about CAD 282 to 293 at 1.35 to 1.40 CAD/USD [A, rate not verified]. Put everything in CAD |
| Billed rate vs total hours | Fractional rate counts billed hours; website line is charged for preview hours | Apply the same acquisition, proposal and admin hours to every line, or none |

| Micro-firm denominator used against automation | 10.7% figure | Use the band you intend to sell to |
| Missing purchase-rate data treated as neutral for growth | research-growth s2a | Symmetric burden: no line has a measured purchase rate for the offer proposed |

**Possible proof weakness to check, not assert:** the one completed website client shares the founder's surname. If the client is family or came through a personal relationship, it is real delivery proof but not evidence of arm's-length demand or of the acquisition channel. The acquisition route is already listed as unknown; resolve it before any lane leans on "we have a local client."

## 7. The facts that would most change the recommendation

| # | Fact | Why it flips things | What a fair experiment must measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Founder's income target, weekly hours, and deadline | A modest target with limited hours favors a few local deals or one retainer; a high target with a near deadline favors whichever lane closes first | Ask directly before the test. Not a market measurement |
| 2 | Real depth of the B2B network | If fewer than a handful of reachable contacts hold budget authority now, the primary collapses | Before outreach: count contacts with current buying authority at funded or revenue-generating firms. During: reply rate, meetings booked, proposals, paid deposits, per contact attempted |
| 3 | Why the prior consultancy ended, and whether the founder wants that work | Fit and enjoyment can override favorable economics | Founder self-report; log energy/enjoyment per lane if he agrees |
| 4 | Actual founder hours per engagement type, including acquisition | Decides whether Grow is a trap, whether previews pay back, and real cash per hour for retainers | Time log by category (prospecting, discovery, proposal, delivery, revisions, reporting, admin, support) for every lane, same categories, same cap |
| 5 | Whether the local site depresses B2B conversion | Decides whether a separate B2B presence is needed before scaling | Record per B2B prospect: did they view the site, did they raise it, did price anchoring come up |
| 6 (secondary) | How the one website client was acquired | Changes how much "local proof" is worth | Founder answer; no outreach needed |

**Fairness rules for the experiment:**
- **Equal acquisition-hours cap per lane**, not equal conversation counts. Warm network conversations are cheaper to get than local ones, so equal counts confound channel warmth with service demand.
- **Separate service from channel.** Offer automation discovery to B2B network contacts too, and content/website discovery to local warm contacts, so one lane is not "best service plus best channel" against "weak service plus cold channel."
- **Success = cash collected (or signed deposit) minus direct costs per total founder hour**, with delivery-hour logs. Not replies or meetings.
- **Distinguish reach failure from offer rejection.** Log contacts attempted, contacts reached, offers presented, and explicit rejections with stated reason.
- **Same currency (CAD), same churn and window** when comparing retainers with one-time fees.

## 8. Verdict

**Primary: modify. Confidence: low.**
Keep the B2B network lane, but (a) name it honestly as a separate offer test (B2B strategic content, not "growth marketing"), (b) gate it: do the network-depth count and a first round of warm outreach before calling it the sales priority, (c) price from content-specific evidence, not the USD 209/hr leadership average, and state the price as an assumption, and (d) let automation be offered inside that same lane to 10 to 99 staff tech firms, since the buyers overlap and automation has the better transaction trend. If the network probe produces paid conversations, promote it. If not, websites becomes primary.

**Alternative: keep websites, modify the framing. Confidence: medium.**
It is the only line with in-brand client proof and a finished storefront. Drop "fastest time to cash" until preview conversion and hours are measured. Test a higher one-time build price in a private offer (local comparators support CAD 2,500+). Resolve the acquisition route of the one client before leaning on it.

**Automation: keep as not the standalone local acquisition priority, but for a narrower reason. Confidence: medium.**
Not because small firms rarely buy (the relevant band buys more), but because local search demand is near zero, two priced competitors already work Niagara, and there is no measured ROI. Test it through the B2B lane and as an add-on, not as its own funnel.

**Local SMB growth packages (Launch/Grow/Dominate as marketing retainers): should not be primary. Confidence: medium.** Weakest evidence of anything studied: flat budgets, collapsing price floor, high directional churn, about 100 local searches/mo, and unknown delivery hours that may make Grow unprofitable at volume.
