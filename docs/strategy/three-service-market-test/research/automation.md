# AI automation line: market research (Well and Good Growth)

Researcher line: AI automation. Date of research: 2026-09-25. All URLs accessed 2026-09-25 unless noted. Evidence labels used: [ADOPTION] official or association survey, [SEARCH] keyword demand, [SELLER] supplier marketing claim, [PRICE] advertised price, [TRANSACTION] marketplace or public-company results, [PROGRAM] government program status, [RESEARCH] analyst or academic study. Assumptions are marked [ASSUMPTION]. Nothing here is a measured Well and Good Growth result; the business has no measured client automation ROI.

## 1. Buyer and triggers

**Buyer problem.** Owners and small teams spend staff time on repeatable admin: lead intake and routing, reply drafting, research or prep briefs, moving data between tools, report assembly. The documented use cases that dominate official data are data analytics (36.6%), text analytics (34.5%) and virtual agents or chatbots (28.2%) among Canadian AI-using businesses (StatCan Q2 2026). Among US small-business workers who use AI, 64% use it for personal productivity (drafting, summarizing) and only 6% for workflow automation (US Chamber Foundation, May 2026). Read together: most current SMB AI use is individual chat use, not connected workflows. That gap is the service opportunity and also the warning sign (buyers may not yet perceive the need).

**Triggers (inferred, not measured).** [ASSUMPTION] Hiring pressure or a staff departure; a lead-response problem; a new tool rollout (CRM, Workspace, Microsoft 365) that exposes manual steps; an owner who has used ChatGPT or Claude personally and wants it "in the business"; a website refresh that surfaces intake gaps (this matches the one Well and Good Growth project example). Supporting signals: BDC's CEO framed SMB time scarcity as the barrier ("SMEs are stretched thin", BDC LIFT release, 2026-04-24); StatCan Q2 2026 found 44.4% of AI-using businesses changed training or staffing practices due to AI.

**Decision-maker.** In owner-led micro firms: the owner, often the same person who would use the output. In 10 to 99 staff firms: owner or GM, with an operations or office manager as champion and whoever administers Microsoft 365, Google Workspace or the CRM as a gatekeeper (often an outsourced MSP). [ASSUMPTION, no survey found on AI purchase authority in SMBs.]

**Realistic first engagement (observed in supplier offers, section 3).** Almost every supplier with published pricing leads with a paid diagnostic: CAD 500 to 2,500 audits, a CAD 1,000 or 2,500 on-site strategy day, a CAD 2,500 two-week assessment credited to a build. Then a fixed-scope single-workflow build (CAD 5,000 to 25,000; USD 1,500 to 15,000). Then an optional retainer (CAD 500 to 10,000 per month; USD 3,500 per month in one US case). No evidence found on how often diagnostics convert to builds or builds to retainers.

**Substitutes (what the buyer can do instead).**
- Built-in AI in tools already paid for: Google Workspace Business Standard includes Gemini across Gmail, Docs, Meet at CA$18.40/user/month list (workspace.google.com, observed 2026-09-25); Microsoft 365 Copilot Business reported at USD 21/user/month (USD 18 promotional) for firms up to 300 users (secondary sources only, Microsoft page timed out); HubSpot Breeze agents now outcome-priced at USD 0.50 per resolved conversation and USD 1 per recommended lead on Pro and Enterprise (HubSpot, 2026-04-14); Zapier includes "AI by Zapier" and Copilot on paid plans from USD 19.99/month annual, Agents add-on from about USD 33/month (zapier.com/pricing).
- Team AI plans: Claude Team USD 20/seat/month annual (USD 25 monthly), including connectors and Claude Cowork (claude.com/pricing); ChatGPT Business USD 20/seat annual, USD 25 monthly, two-seat minimum (secondary reporting of OpenAI help center; direct fetch returned 403).
- Agent platforms sold direct to owners: Lindy from USD 29.99/month (lindy.ai/pricing).
- DIY by the owner or a technical staff member.
- Offshore or marketplace freelancers: Upwork n8n/Zapier rates reported at USD 25 to 100/hour for most work (low-quality secondary sources; Upwork pages returned 403).
- Larger consultancies and MSPs (Microsoft partners bundling Copilot rollouts).
- Doing nothing: 52.7% of Canadian businesses have no plans to use AI in the next 12 months (StatCan Q3 2026).

## 2. Purchase evidence (labelled by type)

**[ADOPTION] Canada, StatCan Canadian Survey on Business Conditions (CSBC).**
- Q2 2026 (collected 2026-04-01 to 05-06; 9,251 respondents of 21,105 sampled; question: used AI "to produce goods or deliver services" in the past 12 months): 19.2% of businesses used AI, up from 6.1% in Q2 2024. 1 to 4 employees: 19.9%. 100+ employees: 27.8%. 40.0% said AI is not relevant to their operations. Barriers among all businesses: cybersecurity or privacy concerns 13.4%, cost 10.6%.
- Q2 2026, external help (denominator: AI-using businesses in each size band): 30.2% of AI-using firms with 100+ employees used external consultants or vendors vs 10.7% of AI-using firms with 1 to 4 employees. 32.0% of AI users trained existing employees. This is the single best official signal on willingness to pay for outside AI help, and it says small firms mostly do not buy it.
- Q3 2026 (collected 2026-07-02 to 08-06, employer businesses): 25.2% plan to use AI in the next 12 months (up from 14.5% in Q3 2025 and 10.6% in Q3 2024). 52.7% have no plans; of those, 79.1% say AI is not relevant, 10.8% cite privacy or security, 9.9% cite lack of knowledge of AI capabilities. No size breakdown published in The Daily release.
- Caveat: "produce goods or deliver services" wording likely undercounts back-office and admin use, which is where automation services sit.

**[ADOPTION] Canada, CFIB (published 2026-04-02).** Surveys: Digital Technology and AI Adoption (2025-04-24 to 06-06, n=1,683) and Your Voice (2026-02-05 to 02-25, n=1,379). Nearly 45% of businesses use GenAI in operations; 39% of firms under 5 employees vs 60%+ at 20 to 49. For 2026: 27% plan to increase AI spending, 52% report no AI investment plans. CFIB membership is not a random sample of all businesses; wording ("use GenAI in operations") is broader than StatCan's, which explains the higher rate.

**[ADOPTION] Canada, BDC (LIFT release 2026-04-24).** Only 30% of Canadian SMEs used AI in 2025; AI users had 24% higher productivity (BDC's own analysis, correlation not causation). Federal "AI for All" strategy (2026-06-04) cites business AI adoption of "just over 12%" with a 60% target by 2034 (pm.gc.ca). Three Canadian adoption figures (12%, 19 to 25%, 30%, 45%) differ because of wording and samples; do not mix them.

**[ADOPTION] US.** Census BTOS (story 2026-05-26; collection 2025-12-14 to 2026-05-03; question revised Nov 2025 to AI use "in any business function" in the past two weeks): 17 to 20% of businesses used AI; 37% at 250+ employees, 32% at 100 to 249, under 20% at firms with fewer than 20 employees with no significant change. US Chamber Foundation Main Street AI Monitor (Ipsos KnowledgePanel, 2026-05-08 to 05-11, n=1,070 workers at firms with 2 to 499 employees): half use AI at work; of users, 6% use it for workflow automation; about 1 in 10 were offered formal AI training; barriers: privacy or security 47%, unclear business application 41%, skills gaps 41%.

**[ADOPTION] OECD, Generative AI and the SME Workforce (2025; 2024 survey of 5,000+ SMEs in seven countries including Canada).** GenAI used in 31% of SMEs. 14% said GenAI reduced reliance on external contractors; 6.3% said it increased reliance (for example to build a custom platform or get compliance advice). Net effect on outside-help demand is negative in this data. Figures from OECD search excerpts; full report pages returned 403.

**[SEARCH] Keyword demand (DataForSEO Google Ads, Sep 2025 to Aug 2026, already collected).** Canada: "ai consultant" 1,000/month, "ai automation agency" 480, "chatgpt for business" 390, "ai for small business" 260, "business process automation" 90, "ai automation services" 50, "workflow automation consulting" 10, "zapier consultant" 10; Niagara, Welland and "near me" variants had no data (too low to report). US volumes run roughly 8 to 10 times Canada on matching terms ("ai automation agency" 4,400; "chatgpt for business" 14,800). Interpretation: national Canadian demand for the specific service is small, and the local variant is effectively zero search volume. "chatgpt for business" and "ai for small business" are likely tool or education intent, not buyer intent for custom builds. "ai agency" (8,100) is ambiguous and not usable. Searching pricing terms for this report returned dozens of vendor-written "AI automation agency pricing 2026" pages, indicating heavy seller competition for the few buyer queries.

**[SELLER] Supplier claims (low quality).** ChatGPT.ca (Markham) claims "50+ automation implementations" and clients "saving an average of 15+ hours per week"; BotLogix claims wineries recover "5-10 hours of weekly admin"; Parix.ai claims benchmarks from "200-plus" projects; Brothers Automate claims retainer clients "ship 8-12 working systems in their first year". None are independently verifiable.

**[PRICE] Advertised prices.** See section 3. Summary: Canadian diagnostic CAD 500 to 2,500; Canadian single-workflow build CAD 5,000 to 25,000; Canadian support CAD 500 to 10,000+ per month; US hourly USD 250 (XRay); US scoped builds USD 1,500 to 15,000 and a USD 3,500/month retainer (Brothers Automate). Most suppliers do not publish build prices.

**[TRANSACTION] Marketplace and public-company results.**
- Upwork Q2 2026 (SEC 8-K, 2026-08-10): total GSV USD 966.4M, down 4% YoY; GSV from AI-related work up more than 22% YoY; GSV from AI Strategy and Consulting up more than 50% YoY; 763,000 active clients; Business Plus (SMB offering) GSV up 174% YoY. Secondary coverage puts AI-related GSV near USD 330M (not in the release text I retrieved). Global, not Canada-specific; AI-related work includes development, not only SMB automation.
- Fiverr Q2 2026 (2026-07-29): revenue USD 97.8M, down 10%; marketplace revenue down 15.5%; annual active buyers 2.7M, down 21.9%; spend per buyer USD 368, up 15.6%; guidance cut for "persistent weakness across categories most exposed to AI automation". Secondary coverage cites programming and tech implementation demand up 94% and marketing workflows up 62% (not verified in primary text).
- Reading: demand is shifting from small gigs to larger AI-integration projects, and low-value transactional work is being absorbed by AI itself. This supports paid AI consulting as a growing category, but globally and via marketplaces where Canadian solo sellers compete on price with offshore talent.

**[PROGRAM] Government money that could fund buyers (status matters).**
- BDC LIFT (launched 2026-04-24): CAD 500M, 1,000+ SMEs, BDC's own AI advisors paired with owners, then loans of CAD 25,000 to 5M. Appears to use BDC advisors, not third-party consultants; could still create downstream implementation demand. [ASSUMPTION]
- OCI Digital Modernization and Adoption Plan (DMAP): open, up to CAD 15,000 for a plan with a Digital Adoption Consultant, Ontario for-profit SMEs with 1 to 499 employees. The DAC roster is "temporarily closed" to new consultants; SMEs can seek approval for an outside consultant through OCI staff. DACs must be Ontario-based, vendor-neutral and arm's length. The implementation stream (Technology Demonstration Program, up to CAD 50,000, CAD 750K revenue minimum) is closed.
- FedDev Ontario RAII: closed (last intake 2024-10-22 to 12-20); next intake not announced, though the national strategy references expanded RAII funding.
- NRC IRAP AI Assist: page last modified 2024-10-22; focuses on SMEs developing GenAI or deep learning, not buying workflow automation; status unclear.
- CDAP: fully closed (wound down 2025-03-31), no direct successor.

**Addressable revenue.** Not estimable from evidence. No source sizes the Canadian or Niagara SMB AI automation services market. [ASSUMPTION] A bottom-up estimate would need counts of Niagara and GTA firms with 5 to 99 staff multiplied by an unknown purchase rate; the only official purchase-rate proxy is 10.7% to 30.2% of AI-using firms using outside consultants or vendors, and AI-using firms are 19 to 28% of businesses in StatCan's measure. That implies roughly 2 to 8% of businesses bought any outside AI help in 12 months, before filtering for automation specifically. Treat as an order-of-magnitude reading, not a forecast.

**[RESEARCH] Failure and abandonment evidence (all with caveats).**
- MIT NANDA, The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025 (Aug 2025): about 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact; purchased or partnered solutions succeeded about 67% of the time vs about one-third for internal builds; biggest ROI in back-office automation. Caveats: enterprise focus, not peer reviewed, success defined as measurable P&L within about six months, sample reported inconsistently across coverage (52 organizations and 153 leaders in some accounts, 150 interviews and 350 employees in Fortune), and widely criticized (Futuriom). The 67% "buy beats build" figure is self-reported and correlational, and it is a convenient claim for any vendor, including this one.
- RAND, Root Causes of Failure for AI Projects (2024): more than 80% of AI projects fail, about twice non-AI IT; based on interviews with 65 data scientists and engineers; leadership misunderstanding of the problem is the top cause. Covers ML projects broadly, not SMB workflow automation.
- Gartner (2025-06-25): over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 due to cost, unclear value or weak risk controls. A prediction from a webinar poll of 3,412 attendees, not an observed rate.
- None of these measure small-business Zapier/Make/n8n-style workflow projects. No SMB-specific abandonment rate was found.

## 4. Channels

- **Warm local network (Niagara).** Highest trust, lowest cost. Competes with Flowgrammer and BotLogix, which both market on-site strategy days to Niagara. Local volume is thin: no measurable search volume for local automation terms. [ASSUMPTION] Best used as referral from website and marketing clients, where the owner already trusts Matthew.
- **Wider professional network (tech startup and B2B contacts from Jetta Grove).** Most plausible buyer pool for a content and growth founder: firms with 10 to 99 staff, existing SaaS stacks and higher AI adoption (information sector 39.7% BTOS use rate in the US). Naturally remote. No evidence on response rates.
- **Outbound.** Permitted under CASL only with implied or express consent or a qualifying exemption; cold email to Canadian businesses is legally constrained [ASSUMPTION based on general CASL rules; not re-researched here]. Competitive: many agencies already produce outbound and SEO content for this service.
- **Partners.** Bookkeepers, accountants and MSPs see manual process pain and control tool access; no data on referral economics. Zapier Solution Partner Program tiers (Certified Expert, then Silver, Gold, Platinum) give directory placement; Canadian partner presence appears low in featured listings, but buyer search for "zapier consultant" in Canada is only 10/month. Make has a tiered partner list (updated 2026-04-08). OCI DMAP could be a funded channel for Ontario-based vendor-neutral advisors, but the DAC roster is closed to new applicants and DACs cannot sell the product they recommend, which conflicts with a plan-then-build model.
- **Marketplaces (Upwork, Fiverr).** Growing AI consulting category on Upwork (+50% AI Strategy and Consulting GSV) but global price competition, platform fees, and Fiverr's shrinking buyer base. Weak fit for positioning as a local trusted advisor.
- **Inbound search.** Canadian national terms exist (ai consultant 1,000/month; ai automation agency 480) but are contested by many content-heavy vendors. Local terms have no volume. Low near-term yield [ASSUMPTION].
- **Workshops and training.** Evidence of a training gap (only about 1 in 10 US small-business workers offered formal AI training; StatCan: 32.0% of AI users trained staff). Niagara Falls Rankin Innovation Hub has hosted small-business AI workshops; Greater Niagara and South Niagara chambers run events mentioning AI. Workshops are a plausible low-risk wedge that fits Matthew's content background, but no pricing or conversion evidence for workshops was found.

## 6. Delivery economics

- **Access and credentials.** Builds require admin access to email, CRM, forms and automation accounts. XRay.Tech's model (client shares screen, no passwords shared, access ends with the call; client owns all accounts and work) and Clarity's rule (software and hosting client-owned) show the market norm: client-owned accounts. This protects the client and limits the supplier's liability, but slows delivery.
- **Review and human approval.** Drafting replies, briefs and record updates for approval (the offer as described) is the safer pattern. Flowgrammer explicitly scopes "human decision points".
- **Failure handling and maintenance.** Workflows break when APIs, app permissions, prompts or models change. Suppliers price this as: 30-day fix guarantee (Brothers Automate), 60 days of optimization support (ChatGPT.ca), paid support tiers covering named workflows only (Clarity, from CAD 500/month), maintenance at 5 to 15% of setup per year or USD 50 to 400/month (Parix.ai, seller benchmark). No independent data on real maintenance hours.
- **Recurring client costs.** Clients pay tools on top of fees: Zapier from USD 19.99/month, Make from USD 12/month, n8n Cloud from EUR 20/month (secondary), AI model usage USD 20 to 200/month per automation (Brothers Automate claim), HubSpot credits, Copilot or Gemini seats.
- **Liability and privacy.** Federal PIPEDA applies to private-sector personal information in Ontario (Ontario has no general private-sector privacy statute; health information falls under PHIPA [ASSUMPTION, general knowledge, not re-verified]). Canadian privacy regulators' GenAI principles (2023-12-07) apply to businesses that use GenAI, stressing purpose, necessity and proportionality and caution in "highly impactful contexts" such as employment and access to finance. OPC issued findings on OpenAI in 2026 (PIPEDA Findings 2026-002). Bill C-36 (introduced 2026-06-15) would replace PIPEDA with the Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act, including automated decision-making provisions; not yet law. Implication: client data flowing into third-party LLM APIs needs documented consent and purpose, and a supplier contract should address data handling and limitation of liability. Professional liability insurance cost not researched.
- **Time to cash.** Diagnostics are 10 business days to 2 weeks; builds 1 to 6 weeks per supplier pages. Sales cycle length: no evidence.
- **Recurring revenue potential.** Every supplier with a full menu offers retainers (CAD 500 to 10,000+ per month; USD 3,500 per month). Retention and churn: no evidence.
- **Scope creep.** Suppliers manage it by limiting support to named workflows and quoting new builds separately (Clarity), or by capping retainer output (Brothers: 1 to 2 automations per month).
- **Adoption risk.** Client may not use what is built. Evidence: RAND and MIT both identify organizational and workflow fit, not technology, as the main failure causes; only 6% of AI-using small-business workers use AI for workflow automation. Training is an adjacent revenue line that also reduces this risk.

## 7. Counterevidence

- **Low relevance perception.** 40.0% of Canadian businesses say AI is not relevant (StatCan Q2 2026); 52.7% have no AI plans and 79.1% of those say it is irrelevant (Q3 2026).
- **Small firms rarely buy outside AI help.** Only 10.7% of AI-using 1 to 4 staff firms used external consultants or vendors (StatCan Q2 2026). OECD: more SMEs say GenAI reduced contractor reliance (14%) than increased it (6.3%).
- **Built-in substitutes are cheap and improving.** Gemini in Workspace Business Standard at CA$18.40/user; Copilot Business about USD 21/user; HubSpot outcome-priced agents; Zapier Copilot included; Claude and ChatGPT team plans at USD 20/seat with connectors. Each makes a "connect my tools" engagement easier to DIY.
- **Freelance market compression.** Fiverr revenue down 10% and buyers down 21.9%; weakness "across categories most exposed to AI automation". Low-end automation work is itself being automated.
- **Hard-to-prove ROI and failure narratives.** MIT NANDA 95% no-P&L-impact and RAND 80%+ failure headlines are widely known among buyers even if methodologically weak; Gartner predicts 40%+ agentic project cancellation. Well and Good Growth has no measured automation ROI to counter this; its diagrams are illustrative.
- **Maintenance drag.** Every build creates a support obligation that competes for a solo founder's time with website and marketing delivery.
- **Crowded local field.** At least two GTA or Burlington firms (Flowgrammer, BotLogix) explicitly market to Niagara with published entry prices (CAD 1,000 to 2,500), fractional offers and on-site days.
- **Trust and privacy.** Privacy or security is the top barrier in StatCan (13.4%) and US Chamber data (47%).

## 8. Geography

- **Delivery is naturally remote.** Flowgrammer states primarily remote delivery; XRay.Tech is remote only; all tools are cloud SaaS.
- **Local presence still sells the first step.** Both local competitors price an on-site strategy day (BotLogix CAD 1,000; Flowgrammer CAD 2,500 + expenses), which suggests owner-led firms value in-person discovery and interviews. [ASSUMPTION: no buyer survey found on local preference for AI services.]
- **Ontario-specific funding requires local status.** OCI DMAP consultants must be Ontario-based; this is a local advantage if the roster reopens.
- **Canada vs US demand.** US search volume is 8 to 10 times Canada on matching terms; US price points are published in USD (USD 250/hour; USD 3,500/month retainer; USD 1,500 to 15,000 builds). US buyers are reachable remotely, but the founder would compete with a far larger US supplier base with no US proof. Canadian prices cluster in CAD at similar nominal numbers to US USD prices, meaning US work pays about 35 to 40% more per nominal dollar [ASSUMPTION: at roughly 1.35 to 1.40 CAD per USD, exchange rate not verified today].
- **Privacy law differs.** Serving US clients adds state privacy law exposure; Canadian clients are under PIPEDA now and possibly the PPCDA later.

## 10. Weakest evidence and biggest unknowns

**Weakest evidence used**
1. All outcome claims (hours saved, systems shipped, payback periods) are seller marketing with no verification.
2. MIT NANDA figures (95%, 67% vs one-third) are enterprise-focused, not peer reviewed and inconsistently described; they cut both ways and should not be quoted as SMB facts.
3. Microsoft Copilot Business and ChatGPT Business prices came from secondary sources after primary pages failed to load.
4. Upwork freelancer rate ranges and SOC 2 costs come from interested vendors or blogs.
5. The addressable-share arithmetic in section 2 (roughly 2 to 8% of businesses buying any outside AI help) combines separate StatCan figures and is an order-of-magnitude reading only.

**Biggest unknowns**
1. Conversion rates from diagnostic to build to retainer; no public data.
2. Sales cycle length and win rates for a solo founder with one internal project example and no measured client ROI.
3. Real maintenance hours per workflow per month after launch.
4. Whether Niagara owner-led firms will pay for automation as a standalone service, or only as an add-on to a website or marketing engagement (the local search volume is effectively zero and small-firm vendor use is 10.7% of AI users).
5. Whether OCI DMAP reopens its consultant roster, and whether RAII's next intake funds SMB workflow adoption at a scale that reaches Niagara firms.
