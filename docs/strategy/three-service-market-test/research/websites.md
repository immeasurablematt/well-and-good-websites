# Market research: WEBSITES line (Well and Good Growth)

Researched 2026-09-25. Read-only web research. All prices are as advertised on the date observed; nothing here is a transaction record unless labelled (e). Labels used: (a) adoption stat, (b) search interest, (c) seller claim, (d) advertised price, (e) transaction evidence, (f) addressable revenue, [A] assumption, [NE] no evidence found.

## 1. Buyer and triggers

**Buyer problem.**
- Credibility and findability for owner-led businesses that lack time. CFIB (Canada, Sept 2025, n=2,478): 66% of owners say lack of time is the greatest obstacle to using digital tools; 78% have a website, 70% among firms with 0 to 4 employees. (a)
- CIRA polling (via search snippet, CIRA page blocked to fetch): 53% of Canadians say a website makes a business look more credible; 30% say they do not trust businesses without one. (a, secondary read; CIRA sells .CA domains so is an interested party)
- Among firms with no digital presence, 55% said digital tools were not relevant to their customers and 38% said there was no real need (CFIB). This is the core objection for the "no website" segment. (a)

**Purchase triggers.** Evidence is thin; most of this is inference.
- New business launch: Clutch (US, n=406, Aug 2025): 12% of small businesses launched a site in the past year. (a)
- Platform cost change: Squarespace raised prices up to 26% from 6 July 2026 (Core annual $23 to $29 USD/mo). Squarespace forum threads show owner complaints. Whether this drives switching to a designer is [NE].
- Rebrand, outdated site, falling enquiries, new service line: [A] common seller narratives; no survey found quantifying these triggers.
- Grants as a trigger are gone for now: CDAP closed to new applicants (program ended 31 Mar 2025); Digital Main Street Ontario $2,500 grant portal says "now closed". (a, program status)

**Decision-maker.** In 0 to 4 employee firms, the owner [A]; no source found that measures who signs off. CFIB respondents are owners.

**Realistic first engagement** [A, inferred from advertised prices in section 3]: a scoped small build or redesign in the $1,500 to $3,500 CAD band locally, or a monthly plan with low setup. Local Niagara floors: Niagara Web Design $1,750 + $50/mo (3 pages); Vision Web Design and Cool Koala both "from $2,500". Well and Good's Launch ($750 setup + $199/mo) sits below local project floors on upfront cost but above US subscription sites on monthly cost.

## 2. Purchase evidence (labelled by type)

**(a) Adoption stats**
- Canada: 78% of small businesses have a website; 70% of 0 to 4 employee firms; 91% of 50+ staff firms. Only 33% of those with sites use them for direct sales. Nearly half name the website their most useful channel, but only about one third of firms aged 2 to 4 years do. (CFIB, Sept 11 to 25 2025, n=2,478, published 17 Dec 2025; CFIB member panel, not a random sample.)
- Canada, official: Statistics Canada's "Enterprises with a website" table (22-10-0016-01) is inactive (2012 to 2013 data). The 2023 SDTIU release does not report website share. No 2025 SDTIU release found. [NE for a current StatCan figure]
- US: 83% of small businesses have a website (up from 64% in 2018); 17% offline; 41% of sites on DIY builders (Wix, Squarespace), 34% WordPress or Shopify, 12% custom; 45% outsourced build, 37% in-house team, 8% owner. (Clutch, SurveyMonkey panel, n=406 US owners, 5 Aug 2025.) Clutch is a directory that sells agency leads (interested party).
- US government source (Census, SBA) on website adoption: [NE]. Third-party "27% to 36% without a website" figures circulate on vendor blogs without traceable methodology; not used.

**(b) Search interest** (DataForSEO keyword data supplied, Sep 2025 to Aug 2026 avg monthly; interpretation only)
- DIY intent dwarfs service intent. Canada: "website builder" 9,900 and "squarespace" 90,500 vs "web design" 6,600. US: "website builder" 201,000 vs "web design" 49,500 (about 4 to 1).
- Local Niagara service searches are small: "web design niagara" 210 + "st catharines" 90 + "welland" 40 = about 340/mo, vs "web design toronto" 4,400.
- Small-business-specific service searches: Canada 210, US 1,900 (about 9x). "Website redesign": Canada 390, US 2,900.
- Caveat: search volume is not purchase volume; multiple local suppliers already target "Niagara web design" pages (Vision, Cool Koala, Cedar + Mint, Niagara Web Design, KMK).

**(c) Seller claims**
- Forge Web (Ontario, blog 1 Apr 2026): Ontario small-business sites "range from about $800 to $10,000+"; recommends "$1,000 to $3,000" build plus "$100 to $150/month" maintenance.
- CanadaWebPro blog: "most small business websites range from $1,899 to $3,899" (conflicts with its own current pricing page, see section 3).
- Hello Web Designs (US, 12 Jul 2026): SMB monthly website spend $16 to $300; all-inclusive monthly plans $100 to $200 USD.
- Hostinger: 93% to 95% of its AI Builder users are first-time site builders (vendor data, Apr 2025 to Mar 2026).
- Thryv: clients on new AI platform see "40% more revenue" (vendor claim, unaudited).

**(d) Advertised prices**: see section 3 table.

**(e) Transaction evidence**
- Wix Q2 2026 (4 Aug 2026): revenue $563.1M USD (+15%); Partners revenue (agencies, freelancers, B2B partners) $213.8M (+17%), outpacing total. Partners revenue grew from $469M (2023) to $750M (2025). Management flagged "Partners softness" in bookings. Interpretation: paid professional builds on DIY platforms are still growing at scale, but Partners includes B2B resellers (Vistaprint, LegalZoom), not only agencies.
- GoDaddy Q2 2026: Applications and Commerce revenue $514.8M USD (+11%); 20.5M customers; ARPU $250. Airo (AI builder/assistant) bookings run rate $50M, 5x the prior quarter. FY2025 10-K: customer retention about 85% in each of five years; about 90% for customers over three years' tenure; 20.4M paying customers; over 2.0M spent more than $500/yr.
- Squarespace (private since Oct 2024, Permira): 5M+ unique subscriptions (Permira portfolio page); July 2026 price rise. No audited results since going private.
- Fiverr FY2025: 3.1M annual active buyers (down 13.6%), spend per buyer $342 USD (+13.3%), revenue $430.9M. No website-category breakdown found.
- Upwork Q2 2026: GSV $966M; 763,000 active clients; GSV per active client $5,230; SMB "Business Plus" GSV +174% YoY (small base; secondary summaries of the release).
- Clutch (US self-report): most professional builds $3,000 to $12,000 USD; 61% spent under $10,000. Self-reported survey, not transactions.
- Squarespace Circle State of the Web Design Industry 2026 (n=1,034 designers, 20+ countries, Nov 2025): lowest-priced projects averaged $2,160 USD, highest averaged $8,179 USD; average designer gross revenue "just shy of $50,000 USD per year". Designer self-report.

**(f) Addressable revenue** (arithmetic on sourced inputs; every step beyond the inputs is [A])
- Niagara Employment Inventory 2025 counted 13,728 visible businesses (excludes home-based). If CFIB's national 22% no-website rate applied (it may not; CFIB panel skews to members) that is about 3,000 businesses without a site; CFIB suggests over half of no-presence firms see no need. The roughly 10,700 with sites are the redesign pool, but redesign frequency and win rates are [NE], so no revenue figure is stated.
- Capacity-based ceiling instead: the average Circle designer grosses about $50,000 USD/yr from web work, which is a realistic benchmark for a solo website-only practice. [A that Circle designers are comparable]

## 6. Delivery economics

- **Revision and support burden.** Scope creep is the second-highest challenge for Circle designers (37%). Typical build time: one week to one month (Circle); local studios quote 2 to 8 weeks. Quantified revision hours: [NE].
- **Time to cash.** Deposits of 25% to 50% are commonly recommended (secondary blogs, no primary survey found). Productized sellers either charge setup ($399 to $1,999 USD) or require 12-month minimums with $0 down (ITsPaul, Golden Coast). Minimist offers 3 or 6 instalment plans. Actual payment delay data: [NE].
- **Recurring revenue.** Admin Bar 2026: agencies with 0% recurring revenue were rarely or never profitable 58.3% of the time; at 25%+ recurring that fell below 10%. Care plan prices observed: $50/mo (Niagara Web Design, bundled), $100 to $150/mo (Forge), $149 to $189/mo (Minimist+), $99/mo (Lakeview Brands, search snippet only; page 404).
- **Churn/retention.** No published churn data for small-agency care plans or website subscriptions: [NE]. Proxies only: GoDaddy about 85% annual customer retention (all products; about 90% after three years); Squarespace previously disclosed about 85% net revenue retention (SaaStr secondary, pre-2024, not re-verified).
- **Commoditization from AI.** Wix Harmony free on all plans (Jan 2026); Squarespace Blueprint; GoDaddy Airo $50M bookings run rate (Q2 2026); 75.4% of US web designers call AI competition challenging and 76% cite AI as their biggest future concern (20i, n=500). Counter-signal: Circle designers using AI to build report 21% higher revenue (US), and Wix Partners revenue still grew 17%.

## 7. Counterevidence

1. The no-website segment largely does not want one: 55% of Canadian firms without digital presence say it is not relevant, 38% no real need (CFIB).
2. DIY dominates: 41% of US small-business sites are on DIY builders and 46% are built in-house or by the owner (Clutch). DIY search intent is about 4x service intent in the US data.
3. AI builders are now free or near free at entry (Wix Harmony on free plan; Hostinger from $2.99; Airo from $9.99), compressing the low end where micro-businesses buy.
4. One-time revenue and low price points hurt profitability (Admin Bar $5k cliff; 58.3% of no-recurring agencies rarely profitable). Average Circle designer grosses only about $50k USD.
5. Local market is crowded and small: at least five Niagara suppliers with published SEO pages; about 340 local searches/mo. Grant-driven demand (CDAP, Digital Main Street) is closed.
6. Young firms value the website less: only about a third of 2 to 4 year old firms cite it as most effective (CFIB), and social/GBP are near-universal substitutes (59% Facebook, 52% GBP).
7. Fiverr buyer count fell 13.6%, and freelance coding/writing job posts fell 21% post-ChatGPT (Demirci et al.), consistent with shrinking low-end paid demand.

## 8. Geography

- **Local identity.** Over 40% of Circle designers work mainly with local clients and over a third work nationally (Circle 2026). All Niagara competitors lead with "Niagara" branding, so local identity is table stakes, not a differentiator [A]. Evidence that a local identity hurts remote sales: [NE].
- **Remote delivery.** Feasible and common: CanadaWebPro, Forge, Minimist, Axiom and B12 sell remotely; Cool Koala serves GTA "and beyond".
- **USD pricing.** US subscription offers cluster at $79 to $199 USD/mo; B12 at $199 USD/mo + $1,999 setup; Circle lowest-project average $2,160 USD. US search volumes are 7.5x (web design) to 9x (small business website design) Canada's.
- **Tax.** CRA: GST/HST small supplier threshold is $30,000 of worldwide taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies; most services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated when exported (RC4022). Conditions for zero-rating specific services not verified. Payment processing: not researched.

## 10. Weakest evidence and biggest unknowns

**Weakest evidence**
- No current Statistics Canada website-adoption figure; Canadian adoption rests on one CFIB member-panel survey.
- US adoption rests on one Clutch panel (n=406) from an interested party; many circulating "no website" stats lack methodology.
- Several supplier prices come from search snippets or seller blogs, not pricing pages (ITsPaul, Lakeview, Forge, Fiverr ranges). Cedar + Mint $5,400 could not be verified; CanadaWebPro's June figures no longer match its pricing page.
- Deposit norms come from blogs, not surveys.

**Biggest unknowns**
1. Care plan and website-subscription churn for small providers: no published evidence found.
2. Win rates, sales cycle length and deal sizes for Niagara website buyers: no evidence found; must come from Well and Good's own pipeline.
3. Whether a free preview converts: no evidence found; only one completed client (Frank Baggetta) exists as proof.
4. Redesign frequency among the roughly 78% of firms that already have sites: no evidence found, which blocks any addressable-revenue estimate.
5. Whether AI builders are shrinking paid demand or shifting it to professionals using those builders: evidence points both ways (Wix Partners +17% vs Fiverr buyers -13.6% and 75% of US designers citing AI competition).
