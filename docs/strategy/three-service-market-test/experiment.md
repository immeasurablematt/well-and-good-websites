# Proposed market experiment

Prepared September 25, 2026 by Claude desktop. **Status: designed, not launched.** No prospect has been contacted, no offer has been sent, no ads have been bought, and the website is unchanged. Starting any step below that involves contacting people needs Matthew's separate go-ahead.

Read `analysis.md` first. This file turns its recommendation into a six-week test with a day-60 review. The sample sizes, caps, and thresholds are proposed learning limits chosen for a solo founder's time, not market benchmarks. They can show obvious mismatches; they cannot establish conversion rates.

## What stays fixed

- Brand name, website copy, design, positioning, public prices, and the three service lines.
- Prospects may be pointed to the existing site for background. Offer documents are private PDFs or emails.
- If a prospect asks about location, say truthfully: based in Welland, working with businesses in Niagara and the GTA, and able to work remotely. Do not claim existing clients elsewhere.

## Week 0: preparation (no outreach)

Complete these before any contact. Keep all names, contact details, and money in a private file outside this repository (for example `~/Hermes/Workbench/market-test/`).

1. **Answer four questions privately:** monthly revenue or income target and deadline; hours per week available; why the B2B consultancy work ended and whether you want more of it; how the one website client was won. If the answer to the third question is "I don't want that work", skip the gate and make websites the primary lane.
2. **Count the network.** List B2B tech or software contacts who meet all of these:
   - can approve, or directly recommend, CAD 5,000 or more in marketing or operations spend;
   - work at a company with revenue or recent funding;
   - know you personally or through a direct introduction, and you could message them without it being a cold email.
3. **Apply the gate.** These thresholds are judgment calls, set so that a 10 to 15 conversation test is possible without exhausting the list.

   | Qualified contacts | Decision |
   |---:|---|
   | 15 or more | B2B network lane is primary; websites are the comparison lane |
   | 8 to 14 | Run both lanes with equal acquisition caps; decide the priority at day 60 |
   | Fewer than 8 | Websites are primary; test B2B content and automation only with the contacts you have |

4. **Count local warm contacts the same way:** established service businesses in Niagara or the GTA (roughly 5 to 49 staff, or an owner-operator already paying for marketing) whose owner you know or can be introduced to.
5. **Set up the time log** with the categories below. Every lane uses the same categories.
6. **Draft the offer documents** from the outlines at the end of this file. Drafting is allowed now; sending is not.
7. **Record the consent basis** for every person you plan to contact (existing business relationship, personal relationship, introduction, or conspicuous publication with a note of where the address was published). Commercial electronic messages to Canadians need CASL consent, identification, and an unsubscribe method; US email falls under CAN-SPAM. This is not legal advice; check any cold message before sending.

## Phase 1, weeks 1 and 2: comparable discovery

Goal: expose obvious mismatches in all three lines before committing to a paid-offer test.

| Lane | Who | Substantive conversations | Acquisition-hours cap |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| B2B network | Network contacts from the count | 4 to 5 | 6 h |
| Local websites | Local warm contacts | 4 to 5 | 6 h |
| Automation | Split across both groups above, asked in the same conversations, plus 1 or 2 established local firms (10 to 99 staff) | 4 to 5 | 6 h |

Equal hours, not equal conversation counts, because warm network conversations are cheaper to get than local ones. **Cross service and channel:** ask network contacts about content, automation, and their website; ask local contacts about their website, marketing, and repeated admin. Otherwise the test compares "best service with best channel" against "weak service with cold channel".

Use the same discovery questions everywhere:

1. What is getting in the way of more customers, or eating your team's time, right now?
2. What have you tried, and what did it cost?
3. Who decides on spending for this, and what budget range is realistic?
4. When would you want it solved, and what happens if it waits?
5. What would make it clearly worth paying for?

At the end of a conversation you may describe the relevant offer outline and its price range. Record the reaction.

## Phase 2, weeks 3 to 6: paid-offer test

| Lane | Role | Qualified conversations | Acquisition-hours cap | Delivery limit while testing |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Primary (set by the gate) | Paid-offer test | 10 to 15 | 24 h | Up to 2 paid projects at once |
| Comparison (the other lane) | Directional comparison | 5 to 8 | 12 h | Up to 2 paid projects at once |
| Automation | Quote only when a buyer raises a workflow need | As arises | Counted inside the lane where it came up | 1 workflow build at once |

Total delivery limit: about 15 hours a week of new paid work during the test, adjusted to the hours answer from week 0. Do not sign more than one new Grow or Dominate client during the test unless its monthly delivery hours are being logged.

**Free previews:** in the website lane, prepare at most three during the test, and only after a qualifying conversation. Log every preview hour against the website lane.

## What to record for every prospect

Keep this in the private tracking file, one row per person.

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Lane, service discussed, offer presented, price quoted | Keeps factors separate |
| Buyer type and size, geography (Niagara, GTA, other Canada, US) | Allows a rough local versus non-local comparison |
| Channel and relationship warmth (close, known, introduced, cold) | Separates channel effects from service effects |
| Consent basis | CASL and CAN-SPAM record |
| Status: attempted, reached, qualified, offer presented, proposal sent, deposit paid, lost | Separates reach failure from offer rejection |
| Stated reason for a no, in their words | Shows whether price, timing, trust, or need is the barrier |
| Did they look at the website? Did they raise it? Did price anchoring come up? | Measures the local-site risk for B2B buyers |
| Cash collected, direct costs | The success measure |

**Time-log categories (all lanes):** prospecting, discovery, proposal or preview, delivery, revisions, reporting, admin, support.

## Success measure

Net cash collected (or deposits paid) minus direct costs, divided by all founder hours logged for that lane, in CAD. Replies, meetings, and polite interest are recorded but do not count as success.

## Decision rules at day 60

| Result in the primary lane | Reading | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 2 or more paid engagements, and logged net CAD 100 or more per founder hour (projected over the engagement) | Offer and channel work | Keep as the sales priority for the next quarter |
| 6 or more qualified conversations with the offer presented, but 0 or 1 paid, with the same objection repeated | Offer problem | Change one thing only (price, scope, or format) and run 5 more conversations |
| Fewer than 6 qualified conversations reached within the 24-hour cap | Reach problem, not an offer verdict | Switch priority to the comparison lane, or test a partner channel before judging the offer |
| Cap used, no paid work, and no live proposals | Does not fit now | Stop the lane; the comparison lane becomes the priority |

| Result in the comparison lane (websites) | Action |
|---|---|
| At least one paid build at CAD 3,000 or more, at most 3 previews per paid build, and logged net CAD 70 or more per hour | Credible priority; if the primary failed, promote websites |
| Previews do not convert, or buyers only accept prices below local floors (CAD 1,750 to 2,500) | Keep websites as a service for inbound and referrals, not an acquisition lane |

| Automation signal | Action |
|---|---|
| 2 or more paid diagnostics or sprints, arriving faster than paid content work in the same conversations | Swap the primary offer to automation for the same buyers |
| Interest but no paid work, or buyers plan to use built-in tools | Keep as an add-on for existing clients |

**Outcome window for marketing work:** SEO and content results take months (Google says four months to a year for SEO). This test judges only whether buyers pay and whether delivery economics hold. Judge client outcomes separately, 90 to 180 days after delivery starts, using agreed measures such as enquiries, demo requests, or search clicks.

## Evidence needed before any later change to the site

This test does not change the site. At the review, a later proposal to add B2B or wider-geography copy would need both of the following:

1. At least 2 paid clients from outside Niagara and the GTA, or from the B2B lane.
2. At least 3 prospects who raised the local positioning or package prices as a concern.

If local work wins, or B2B prospects never mention the site, keep the current approach.

## Known confounds

- Warm and cold contacts are not comparable. Report results by warmth.
- Network contacts are mostly B2B and local contacts are mostly owner-led, so buyer size and geography move together. Label this in the review rather than claiming a geography effect.
- One founder runs every lane, so learning in one lane may improve pitches in another.
- Sample sizes are too small for rates. Report counts.

## Draft offer outlines (private collateral, not sent)

### A. Positioning and content sprint (B2B network lane)

- **For:** B2B tech or software teams with a product customers already pay for, a founder or marketing lead who owns the messaging, and budget to approve the fee within 30 days.
- **Problem it solves:** the product has outgrown how it is described; sales conversations, the website, and content say different things.
- **Scope (about 4 weeks):** interviews with the founder and 2 or 3 customer-facing staff; a positioning and messaging document; rewritten copy for the homepage or one key product page, or one sales deck; two customer stories or three long-form pieces; a 90-day content plan.
- **Proposed price:** USD 4,000, or CAD 5,500 for Canadian clients. 50% deposit, balance on delivery. A judgment within the range in `comparison.csv`; test it, do not treat it as a market rate.
- **Follow-on:** monthly content lead at about USD 3,500 a month for a defined output and about 30 hours, 3-month initial term, then month to month.
- **Excludes:** paid ads management, software development, link buying.
- **Proof to show:** the SeamlessFi, io.net, Nulogy, and Top Hat work, labelled as prior consultancy work.
- **Next step:** a 30-minute call, then a written proposal within two business days.

### B. Workflow sprint (automation, offered inside either lane when a buyer raises a need)

- **For:** teams of about 10 to 99 staff with one repeated task that someone owns, such as research briefs, content drafts for approval, or intake and record updates.
- **Scope (about 3 weeks):** map one process from an interview; build and test it in the client's own accounts; agreed human approval points; documentation; 30 days of fixes.
- **Proposed price:** USD 4,000 or CAD 5,000, or a CAD 1,500 diagnostic credited to the build for local firms. Positioned between BotLogix (CAD 1,000 strategy day) and Flowgrammer (CAD 2,500 audit, builds from CAD 7,500).
- **Excludes:** enterprise security certification, work on systems the client does not own, unattended customer-facing sending.
- **Ongoing support:** quoted separately per named workflow.
- **Proof to show:** the interview and project-update workflow, labelled as a project example with no measured savings.

### C. Website rebuild for established service businesses (comparison or primary lane)

- **For:** Niagara or GTA service businesses with an outdated site or one that does not bring enquiries, where the owner decides and a budget of CAD 3,000 or more is realistic.
- **Scope:** 5 to 8 pages, copy written from an interview, mobile checks, a clear enquiry path, Google Business Profile connection, launch, 30 days of fixes.
- **Proposed price:** CAD 3,000 to 4,500 one-time through the existing "one-time builds" route, 50% deposit; optional care plan CAD 100 to 150 a month. The public Launch, Grow, and Dominate packages remain available unchanged.
- **Free preview:** only after a qualifying conversation, maximum three during the test.
- **Proof to show:** the completed client case study and the labelled concept builds.

### D. Local discovery offers (Phase 1 only, if a local buyer asks what a first paid step looks like)

- Local search and content audit with a 90-day plan: CAD 1,500.
- Workflow diagnostic: CAD 1,500, credited to a build.

## Review checklist for day 60

1. Totals by lane: attempted, reached, qualified, offers, proposals, paid, cash, direct costs, hours by category.
2. Net CAD per founder hour by lane.
3. Stated reasons for each no, grouped.
4. Site and geography observations.
5. Decision against the rules above, written as a short non-sensitive summary in this folder. Keep names and amounts private.
