- **Priority: websites for small and medium businesses in one chosen industry, sold remotely to Canada and the US.** The US has roughly 7 to 11 times the search volume on the same terms in this study, and US prices paid in USD are worth about a third more in CAD. With no paying clients anywhere, a lack of US proof is no bigger a gap than a lack of Canadian proof. Record which country responds rather than assuming. The local network still counts, but it is no longer the boundary.
- **The first paid client is the real test.** Every price in `comparison.csv` is untested against an actual buyer. Growth marketing and automation stay available as add-ons for those clients.
- **Why websites, at 10 hours a week:** a build is a bounded project with a deposit, and the site, preview offer, and delivery process already exist. A Grow client at about 12 hours a month would use roughly a third of available time on its own. Automation still has no measured client result.
- **Why one industry:** it gives outreach a list to work from, lets one case study speak to the next buyer, and turns "websites for anyone" into a specific offer. Local search is small in every line, so geography was never the main source of buyers.
- **Open decision:** which industry. The next step is a short comparison of three or four candidate industries using the same evidence template. The experiment in `experiment.md` should be scaled to about 3 hours a week of selling and one project at a time.
- **Positioning:** Matthew is now open to a small positioning change. Test the industry offer with private collateral first. Change the site only after the test produces paying clients in that industry.

## Recommendation

**Initial sales priority: growth marketing for B2B tech and software teams, sold through Matthew's existing professional network.** Start with teams in the GTA, which is inside the stated service area, and accept wider Canadian and US teams that come through the same network. Lead with a fixed-price content and positioning sprint, with a monthly content retainer as the follow-on. In the same conversations, record automation needs and quote a workflow sprint when the buyer raises one.

**Confidence: low to medium, and gated.** This priority holds only if a private count of Matthew's network finds enough contacts who can approve spending now (the threshold is in `experiment.md`). If the count falls short, or the first round of warm conversations produces no paid work within the acquisition-hours cap, the alternative becomes the priority.

**Credible alternative: fixed-scope websites for established local service businesses in Niagara and the GTA, sold through warm relationships and referral partners.** Price one-time builds from about CAD 3,000 in private offers, using the existing "one-time builds are also available" route. Offer the free preview only after a qualifying conversation. **Confidence: medium.**

**Keep available, but do not lead acquisition with:**

1. Launch, Grow, and Dominate as the lead offer for small local businesses. Their evidence is the weakest of anything studied, and Grow may lose money per hour at volume (below).
2. AI automation as a standalone local funnel. Local search demand is effectively zero, two priced competitors already sell to Niagara, and there is no measured client result yet.
3. Cold outreach to US businesses in any line. There is no US proof and no relationship advantage. (Superseded September 26: with an industry focus and remote delivery, the US is included. See the update above.)

## Why this order

### 1. Proof decides who will believe the pitch

| Line | Strongest proof for the buyer being pitched | Proof type |
|---|---|---|
| Growth marketing, B2B tech | Named content, social, and search results for SeamlessFi and io.net, plus Nulogy and Top Hat assets | Prior consultancy experience |
| Websites, local | One completed client website: search clicks rose from 25 to 51 between two 28-day windows, and the client reported three enquiries in the first month | Client proof under this brand |
| Growth marketing, local small business | The same single website client | Client proof, but for a website, not a marketing retainer |
| AI automation | One internal interview and project-update workflow | Project example, no measured savings |

The B2B buyer is the only buyer for whom Matthew has several named, relevant results. Websites are the only line with client proof under this brand. Local marketing retainers and automation have no direct proof for the buyer they target. Proof is not transferred between lines here: the startup content results do not show local SEO results, and the website case does not show automation savings.

Before relying on the one website client as evidence of local demand, confirm how that client was won. A personal relationship would still show delivery quality, but not arm's-length demand or a repeatable channel.

### 2. Economics per founder hour favour the network lanes, if wins happen

Base case, net CAD per founder hour over the first 90 days after signing. Hours include sales time spent on prospects who do not buy and unpaid previews. Prices are observed ranges or current public prices; **all hours are assumptions** until the experiment logs them. US prices are converted at an assumed CAD 1.37.

| Row | Offer | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| G3 | B2B content sprint then retainer, GTA, network | 77 | 130 | 204 |
| A3 | Workflow sprint for B2B tech teams, network | 63 | 137 | 294 |
| A2 | Diagnostic then workflow build, 10 to 99 staff, Ontario | 57 | 122 | 250 |
| W1 | Local website build with optional care plan, warm | 41 | 78 | 164 |
| A1 | Diagnostic then small build, local micro business | 38 | 78 | 181 |
| G2 | Local search and content audit | 30 | 62 | 156 |
| G1 | Grow package, local | 32 | 45 | 69 |
| W2 | Launch package, local, cold outreach with preview | 20 | 31 | 45 |

Three readings matter:

- **The per-hour gap between the network lanes and local websites is large enough to survive conservative pricing.** B2B content is priced here well below fractional-leadership benchmarks, at the challenger's request. Even the project alone, with no retainer months, comes to about CAD 97 per hour in the base case, still above a local build (CAD 78).
- **Automation's per-hour figures look competitive only if wins happen.** A2 and A3 carry the weakest proof and the least evidence on win rates. That makes them a reason to test automation inside the network conversations, not a reason to lead with it.
- **The Grow package is a capacity trap if the delivery-hour assumption is right.** At 12 hours a month for SEO, two content pages, about 12 posts, reviews, and a report, Grow returns about CAD 45 per hour. Every Ontario comparator found charges more for a single channel (CAD 950 to 1,999 a month for social alone). This analysis does not change public prices. It does recommend logging real hours on any Grow or Dominate client before selling more than a few.

### 4. Where buyers can be reached now

| Channel | Evidence | Available now? |
|---|---|---|

Every line depends on relationships more than search. That is why the experiment caps acquisition hours per lane instead of equalizing conversation counts: warm network conversations are cheaper to get than local ones.

## Strongest objections to the recommendation

The cross-line challenge (`research/challenge.md`) argued these, and the recommendation was changed in response.

1. **It may be the old consultancy under a new name.** The analysis does not know why Matthew moved from B2B work to a local business. If the reason was falling demand, burnout, or a real preference for local, owner-facing work, that overrides the per-hour advantage. This is the most important question in the whole study.
2. **The startup funding base is thinner.** Canada recorded its lowest quarterly VC deal count since 2017 in Q1 2026 (CVCA via BetaKit); over 70% of Q2 2026 global startup capital went to AI companies (Crunchbase). Fewer funded non-AI startups means fewer "we just raised" triggers.
3. **Content work is the most commoditized part of marketing.** AI Overviews cut clicks on informational queries sharply (Ahrefs), and written content costs US$0.06 to 0.16 per word from productized suppliers. The offer has to sell positioning, judgment, and sales assets, not article volume.
4. **The public site speaks to local businesses.** A B2B buyer who visits it sees CAD 199 to 1,299 packages. This could lower trust or anchor price. The site stays frozen during the test, so the experiment records whether prospects looked at it and raised it.
5. **Concentration.** Two or three retainers could carry most of the revenue, and startups end engagements when funding runs out or they hire.
6. **One source carried three legs of the original argument.** The Fractional Jobs report supplied the demand, price, and channel evidence. The price leg has been removed; the channel leg remains and is labelled.

## The case for staying local, and against it

**For staying local:** the site, prices, and free preview are already built for local buyers; websites have the only client proof under this brand; local studios publish floors of CAD 1,750 to 2,500 and typical projects of CAD 3,500 to 8,000, so there is room to price builds higher; professional builds on DIY platforms are still growing (Wix Partners revenue up 17% in Q2 2026).

**Against staying local:** local search is small in every line; local small-business marketing budgets look flat to shrinking in public-company data (Yelp, Yext, Thryv); local marketing price floors are very low (CheckSite from CAD 30 a month, Localo from US$39); the average solo web designer in Squarespace's survey grosses about US$50,000 a year; and 55% of Canadian firms without a digital presence say it is not relevant to them (CFIB).

**Local identity versus delivery geography:** no source in any line measured whether a local identity raises close rates. It is kept as a hypothesis. Remote delivery is normal in all three lines, so the question is trust and fit, not logistics. The GTA is both inside the stated territory and the main local search market ("web design toronto" and "seo toronto" each about 4,400 a month).

## Service-line summaries

### Websites

- **Buyer:** established owner-led service businesses replacing a site that does not produce enquiries. Most firms already have a site (78% in Canada, CFIB), so this is a redesign market.
- **Substitutes:** free or near-free AI builders (Wix Harmony, GoDaddy Airo), US$79 to 199 subscription sites, local studios.
- **Economics:** bounded delivery, deposit at signing, low direct costs. Agencies with projects under US$5,000 and no recurring revenue are less often profitable (Admin Bar), so pair builds with a care plan.
- **Biggest unknowns:** preview-to-sale conversion, preview hours, care-plan retention.

### Growth marketing

- **Local small business:** weakest line in the evidence. Budgets flat; price floors very low; SEO takes four months to a year (Google); annual churn estimates of 38% (SEO) and 46% (social) come from a source with undisclosed methods.
- **B2B tech:** best proof and best per-hour economics, but a thinner funding base and an unknown network.
- **AI search visibility:** consumer use is real (45% of US consumers used AI tools such as ChatGPT or Claude to find local businesses, BrightLocal), but AI referrals are still about 1% of site traffic, and no source measures how many Canadian businesses pay for it. Keep it as part of SEO work, not a separate lead offer.

### AI automation

- **Buyer:** teams of 10 to 99 staff have the most use and budget. 30.2% of AI-using firms with 100+ staff used outside consultants or vendors, against 10.7% of AI-using firms with 1 to 4 staff (StatCan Q2 2026).
- **Growth signal:** planned AI use rose from 14.5% to 25.2% of Canadian employer businesses in a year (StatCan Q3 2026); Upwork AI Strategy and Consulting volume grew more than 50% while total volume fell 4%. This is the strongest demand trend in any line.
- **Against:** built-in AI in Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, HubSpot, and Claude or ChatGPT team plans substitutes for simple "connect my tools" work; OECD found more small firms reduced contractor use because of GenAI (14%) than increased it (6.3%); Flowgrammer (audit CAD 2,500) and BotLogix (strategy day CAD 1,000) already sell to Niagara; and every build creates a maintenance obligation.

## Cross-selling, counted once

- Grow and Dominate already include a website. Their revenue is counted once, in the growth rows.
- A website client may later buy marketing or automation. That is recorded as an option, not added to the website row's value.
- In the B2B lane, a content client and an automation client at the same company count as one engagement in the comparison.
- A sensible outcome keeps all three services available for qualified work while pointing acquisition hours at one lane.

## What would change this recommendation

| If this turns out true | Then |

| Matthew does not want to return to B2B content work, or the reason the consultancy ended still applies | Websites become the priority |

| Local warm conversations produce paid builds at CAD 3,000 or more and previews convert at 1 in 3 or better | Websites become the priority, with evidence for a higher build price |
